\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:proof}

\subsection{The usual criterion}
We first recall the standard way of passing from the three properties of the polynomials $Q_N$ to a
measure, to explain where the remaining ideas enter.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:criterion}
Let $\xi\in\R$, $d\in\N$ and $\eta,\rho>0$, and let $Q_p\in\Z[X]$ be polynomials, indexed by primes $p$,
such that for all large $p$: $\deg Q_p\le dp$; $Q_p(a/b)\ne0$ whenever $p\nmid b$; and
$|Q_p(x)|\le e^{-\eta p^2}$ whenever $|x-\xi|\le e^{-\rho p}$. Then for every $\varepsilon>0$,
$\mu(\xi)\le(1+\varepsilon)\rho\max(d/\eta,c/\varepsilon)$ for every $c>1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $|\xi-a/b|$ be small and $X=\max(d/\eta,c/\varepsilon)\log b$. By the prime number theorem
$\theta((1+\varepsilon)X)-\theta(X)=\varepsilon X+o(X)>\log b$, so some prime $p\in(X,(1+\varepsilon)X]$ does not
divide $b$. If $|\xi-a/b|\le b^{-(1+\varepsilon)\rho\max(d/\eta,c/\varepsilon)}$, then $|\xi-a/b|\le e^{-\rho p}$
and the nonzero integer $b^{dp}Q_p(a/b)$ has absolute value at most $b^{dp}e^{-\eta p^2}<1$, since
$p>X\ge(d/\eta)\log b$.
\end{proof}

The loss $\max(d/\eta,c/\varepsilon)$ instead of $d/\eta$ is the cost of finding a prime that does not divide
$b$ in a short interval. We now remove it, using the two remaining properties of $Q_N$: the
nonvanishing under $p^2\nmid b$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:nonvanishing}) and the divisibility of the
coefficients by prime squares.

\subsection{Prime squares in the coefficients}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:recycling}
For all large $N$ and every prime $N<q\le2N$, the $j$-th coefficient of $Q_N$ is divisible by $q^{2j}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For these primes the matrices $q^2\cA^0_N$ and $\cL_N$ are $q$-integral, and $q^{40N}$ divides $D_N$. The
identity
\[
 Q_N(X/q^2)=(18N)!\,\frac{D_N}{q^{40N}}\det\bigl(q^2\cA^0_N+X\cL_N\bigr)
\]
shows that $Q_N(X/q^2)$ has $q$-integral coefficients; its $j$-th coefficient is $c_{N,j}/q^{2j}$.
\end{proof}

Consequently, if $B$ is a product of squares $q^2$ of distinct primes $N<q\le2N$ with $B\mid b$, then every
term $c_{N,j}a^jb^{18N-j}$ of $b^{18N}Q_N(a/b)$ is divisible by $B^{18N}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recycled}
 1\le\Bigl(\frac bB\Bigr)^{18N}\Bigl|\frac{Q_N(a/b)}{\Omega_N}\Bigr|\qquad\text{whenever }Q_N(a/b)\ne0 .
\end{equation}
Here we have also divided by the common factor $\Omega_N$ of Section~\ref{sec:content}, whose primes exceed
$2N$ and therefore do not meet $B$.

\subsection{The choice of the prime}
Put
\[
 \rho=\frac{259299}{2500},\qquad
 \eta=\eta_\infty+\chi=\frac{5993}{3125}+\frac{649}{1000}=\frac{64169}{25000},\qquad
 k=\frac{728}{\rho}=\frac{1820000}{259299},\qquad\delta_0=10^{-7}.
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:real} and Section~\ref{sec:content}, the integer polynomials $\widetilde Q_N=Q_N/\Omega_N$
satisfy $|\widetilde Q_N(x)|\le e^{-(\eta-o(1))N^2}$ for $|x-G|\le e^{-\rho N}$; their degree is at most
$18N$, and they do not vanish at $a/b$ for $N=p$ prime with $p^2\nmid b$.

Let $a/b$ be a rational number with $b$ large, put $L=\log b$, $U=kL$ and $V=(k-1)L$, and suppose
$|G-a/b|\le b^{-728}$. By the prime number theorem, $(1-\delta_0)t\le\theta(t)\le(1+\delta_0)t$ for all large
$t$. If every prime $p\in(V,U]$ had $p^2\mid b$, then $2(\theta(U)-\theta(V))\le L$; but
\[
 2\bigl(\theta(U)-\theta(V)\bigr)\ge2L\bigl(1-\delta_0(2k-1)\bigr)>L .
\]
Let $p$ be the \emph{largest} prime in $(V,U]$ with $p^2\nmid b$. Every prime $q\in(p,U]$ has $q^2\mid b$, and
$q\le U<2V<2p$ because $k>2$. Let $B$ be the product of these $q^2$, so that $\log B=2T$ with
$T=\theta(U)-\theta(p)\ge U-p-2\delta_0U$.

Since $\rho p\le\rho U=728L$, we have $|a/b-G|\le e^{-\rho p}$, and \eqref{eq:recycled} with $N=p$ gives
$\eta p^2\le18p(L-2T)+o(p^2)$. Hence
\[
 \eta p\le18\bigl(L-2U+2p+4\delta_0U\bigr)+o(p).
\]
As $\eta<36$, the left side minus $36p$ decreases in $p$, and $p\le U=kL$ gives
\[
 \eta k\le18+72\delta_0k+o(1).
\]
But
\[
 \eta k-18-72\delta_0k=\frac{513512}{32412375}>0,
\]
a contradiction. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

The choice of the largest admissible prime is what makes the selection free: the primes of the interval
that are not admissible have their squares in $b$, and these squares are recovered from the
coefficients through Lemma~\ref{lem:recycling}. In the limit $\delta_0\to0$ the argument gives the exponent
\[
 \frac{18\rho}{\eta}=\frac{46673820}{64169}=727.357\ldots .
\]

\section{Limits of the method}\label{sec:limits}

The exponent is determined by three numbers: the degree $18$, the margin $\eta$ and the perturbation
exponent $\rho$. The margin is the sum of the difference between the real and the finite places and the
contribution $\chi$ of the common factor. The perturbation exponent is the cost $20c_2$ of a fixed column
plus the logarithmic size $17.913$ of the quadrature weights.

Within the framework of Section~\ref{sec:real}, with the gap radius $g_0=401/2500$, the weight $17.913$ and the
exponent $\chi=0.649$ of the common factor fixed, the integer $728$ is close to optimal. A weak dual certificate on $934$
rational intervals, verified in interval arithmetic at $60$ and $80$ digits, shows that no pair of trial
sequences gives an integer exponent $B\le726$ in this framework; its margin is $3.5\cdot10^{-6}$ at $B=726$
and $-5.3\cdot10^{-6}$ at $B=727$, and finer subdivisions approach $726.5$. This computation is not part of
the formal proof, and it says nothing about $\mu(G)$ itself. Further progress requires new arithmetic,
which enlarges $\chi$ or reduces the degree, or a better treatment of the fixed columns, which reduces $\rho$.

\section{Formalization}\label{sec:formal}

All results of this paper are formalized in Lean~4 \cite{Lean4} with Mathlib \cite{Mathlib}, on top of
OpenAI's formalization of the irrationality of $G$ \cite{OpenAI2026}. The development is available at
\begin{center}
\FormalizationLink.
\end{center}
The statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} in \lean{Challenge.lean} uses only Mathlib, with Catalan's constant
written as the series $\sum_j(-1)^j/(2j+1)^2$; its proof is \lean{Solution.lean}. The solution has no \lean{sorry} and no \lean{native\_decide}, and the command
\lean{\#print axioms} reports only the three standard axioms \lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice} and
\lean{Quot.sound}. The file \lean{comparator.json} is a configuration for \lean{lake comparator}
\cite{Comparator} stating that \lean{Solution} proves the statement of \lean{Challenge} with these axioms
only. We have not run Comparator, which requires Linux; the axiom check above was done with
\lean{\#print axioms}.
The prime number theorem is taken from the PNT+ project \cite{PNTPlus}.

The proof combines conventional formal proofs with numerical certificates checked by the Lean kernel:
the inverses of the base matrices modulo $1009$, the response matrices of Section~\ref{ssec:content1}, the
cover of Lemma~\ref{lem:quadrature}, the trial sequences of Section~\ref{ssec:trials} and the covers of
Section~\ref{ssec:cells}. All are exact rational or integer computations, decided by the kernel without
compiled code. Large certificates are split into many small declarations with explicit intermediate
values. Where a recurrence produces integers divisible by large powers of $2$, as the Chebyshev and Clenshaw
recurrences do, the certificates run on the quotients by these powers, with one lemma relating the two. The
interval arithmetic used to generate the trial data and the covers \cite{Arb} is not trusted: Lean
rechecks every inequality.

The parts of OpenAI's formalization that are used were converted to Lean's module system and ported to
Lean \texttt{v4.35.0-rc2}; the repository's \texttt{README} and \texttt{PORTING.md} describe the changes.
