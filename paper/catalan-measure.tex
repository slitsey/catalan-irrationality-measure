\title{An irrationality measure for Catalan's constant}

\author{Sean Litsey}
\email{sean.litsey.math@gmail.com}

\date{October 23, 2026}

\subjclass[2020]{Primary 11J82; Secondary 11J72, 31A15, 68V20}

\keywords{Catalan's constant, irrationality measure, determinants, logarithmic potentials,
Gauss quadrature, formal verification}

\begin{abstract}
Let $G=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j(2j+1)^{-2}$ be Catalan's constant. Building on OpenAI's recent proof
that $G$ is irrational, we prove that $|G-a/b|>b^{-728}$ for every integer $a$ and every
sufficiently large integer $b$; thus the irrationality exponent of $G$ is at most $728$.
The irrationality proof compares, for a determinant $\Delta$ whose entries are linear forms in
$1$ and $G$, a lower bound coming from the denominators of $\Delta$ with an upper bound coming from an
integral representation. To obtain a measure we regard the determinant as a polynomial
$\Delta_N(X)$ in the constant and bound it, not only at $X=G$, but on an exponentially small
neighbourhood of $G$. We control the derivative of the determinant through a Gauss quadrature formula,
which turns the perturbation into point evaluations of the row functions, and an identity of
Andr\'eief type with fixed points. We use a determinant of size $20N$ rather than
$48N$, and we show that every coefficient of the resulting integer polynomial is divisible by
large powers of the primes in three intervals, by proving rank deficiencies of the reduced
matrix modulo these primes with Chebyshev identities in characteristic $p$. Prime squares in the
denominator of a rational approximation are recovered from the coefficients, which removes the
cost of selecting a good prime. The energy estimates at the real place use trial potentials with
complex poles placed near the edges of the support of the extremal measure. All steps are formalized
in the Lean proof assistant, with only the standard axioms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Catalan's constant
\[
 G=\sum_{j=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^j}{(2j+1)^2}=L(2,\chi_{-4})=0.91596\,55941\,77219\ldots
\]
is the value at $s=2$ of the Dirichlet $L$-function of the nontrivial character modulo $4$. Its
arithmetic has long been studied by analogy with $\zeta(2)$ and $\zeta(3)$, but until recently
it was not known to be irrational. In October 2026 OpenAI \cite{OpenAI2026} proved that $G$
is irrational and verified the proof in Lean. (An earlier claimed proof \cite{Sun2026} was shown
to contain a gap \cite{Wachs2026}.)

The \emph{irrationality exponent} $\mu(\xi)$ of a real number $\xi$ is the infimum of the real
numbers $\mu$ such that $|\xi-a/b|>b^{-\mu}$ for all integers $a$ and all sufficiently large
integers $b$. Every irrational number has $\mu(\xi)\ge2$, and almost every real number has
$\mu(\xi)=2$. An \emph{irrationality measure} is an explicit upper bound for $\mu(\xi)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every integer $a$ and every sufficiently large integer $b$,
\[
 \left|G-\frac ab\right|>b^{-728}.
\]
In particular $\mu(G)\le728$.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses the prime number theorem. The theorem and every result in this paper have been
verified in the Lean~4 proof assistant on top of OpenAI's formalization, with no unproved steps and
only the standard axioms (Section~\ref{sec:formal}).

\subsection{Previous results}
Irrationality proofs for the values of $L$-functions at integers follow, almost without exception,
the pattern of Ap\'ery's proof for $\zeta(3)$ \cite{Apery79}. One constructs integer linear forms
$q_n\xi-p_n$ that tend to zero, with $q_n\ne0$, faster than the growth of their coefficients would allow
for a rational $\xi$. If $|q_n\xi-p_n|=e^{-\alpha n+o(n)}$ and $|q_n|=e^{\beta n+o(n)}$, then
$\xi$ is irrational as soon as $\alpha>0$, and $\mu(\xi)\le1+\beta/\alpha$. Beukers
\cite{Beukers79} gave the linear forms for $\zeta(2)$ and $\zeta(3)$ as integrals of Legendre-type
polynomials. Hata \cite{Hata93} refined such integrals and introduced
the systematic use of the primes that divide all the coefficients of the forms, an idea going back
to Chudnovsky. Rhin and Viola \cite{RhinViola96,RhinViola01} found that families of integrals
for $\zeta(2)$ and $\zeta(3)$ are permuted by a finite group, and used the invariance of the integrals
to divide all their coefficients by a large common factor. This \emph{arithmetic method} gave the
sharpest measures for $\zeta(2)$ and $\zeta(3)$, and Marcovecchio \cite{Marcovecchio09} applied it to
$\log2$. For $\pi$, Zeilberger and Zudilin \cite{ZeilbergerZudilin20} obtained $\mu(\pi)\le7.1032\ldots$
from Salikhov's integrals \cite{Salikhov08} with an additional arithmetic saving.

Since September 2026 a series of new results in this area has appeared, several of them obtained with
AI systems. OpenAI proved $\mu(\pi)=2$, with a Lean formalization \cite{OpenAIPi2026}, and Liu, Jiang and
Zhang proved $\mu(\log r)=2$ for every rational $r>0$, $r\ne1$ \cite{LiuJiangZhang26}. Giraudin lowered
the bounds for $\mu(\zeta(2))$ and $\mu(\zeta(3))$ to $5.0193784$ and $5.5138800$ by applying the
Rhin--Viola method at new integral directions \cite{Giraudin26,Giraudin26z3}. Leibtag, Barkan, Kalisch,
Weinbaum, Shalyt and Kaminer \cite{LBKWSK26} showed that the classical constructions are
one-dimensional trajectories in conservative matrix fields, and by searching over other trajectories
obtained $\mu(\zeta(2))<4.62675$ and $\mu(\zeta(3))<5.4197$. Prellberg \cite{Prellberg26} proved that
$\mathrm{Li}_2(-1/4)$, $\mathrm{Li}_2(1/5)$ and $\mathrm{Li}_2(-1/3)$ are irrational with the integrals of
Rhin--Viola and Viola--Zudilin. Fauzan announced a proof that $\zeta(5)$
is irrational, using Hankel determinants \cite{Fauzan2026}, and Lai combined this method with simultaneous
approximations to show that $1$, $\zeta(3)$ and $\zeta(4)$ are linearly independent over $\Q$
\cite{Lai2026}. Several of these arguments, like the present one, use determinants of large size rather
than single sequences of linear forms; Zudilin's determinantal criterion \cite{Zudilin} is an earlier
instance of this idea.

For Catalan's constant the analogous linear forms exist but do not decay fast enough.
Rivoal and Zudilin \cite{RivoalZudilin2003} proved that at least one of the numbers
$\beta(2),\beta(4),\ldots,\beta(2m)$ is irrational for suitable $m$, where $\beta$ is the Dirichlet beta
function; $G=\beta(2)$ is not singled out. Zudilin \cite{Zudilin2003} found an Ap\'ery-like
recurrence for $G$ whose solutions give linear forms that converge to zero, but too slowly to prove
irrationality, and \cite{Zudilin02Cheb,KrattRivoal08,KrattZudilin19,Zudilin19Survey} describe
related constructions and their limits. Eskandari, Murty and Nemoto \cite{EMN2025} interpret linear forms
in Catalan's constant through mixed motives. Leibtag et al.\ \cite{LBKWSK26} found further Ap\'ery-like
approximations to $G$ in their matrix fields; the best they report falls just short of proving
irrationality.

OpenAI's argument \cite{OpenAI2026} has a different shape. Instead of a sequence of linear forms it
uses a determinant $\Delta$ of size $48N$ whose entries are rational linear combinations of $1$ and
$G$. Determinants of linear forms were used for irrationality in Zudilin's determinantal approach
\cite{Zudilin}; here the entries are double integrals against the Cauchy kernel $1/(1-ts)$. If $G$
were rational, the
denominators of the entries would give a lower bound $|\Delta|\ge e^{-(\mathsf A+o(1))n^2}$ with
$n=48N$. An integral representation, of the kind that appears in random matrix theory through
Andr\'eief's identity \cite{Forrester2018Andreief}, gives the upper bound
$|\Delta|\le e^{-(\mathsf U+o(1))n^2}$, and an energy estimate shows $\mathsf U>\mathsf A$. The margin is
small: $\mathsf U-\mathsf A\approx1.3\cdot10^{-4}$. The nonvanishing of $\Delta$ along the primes
$N=p$ is proved separately by reduction modulo $p$.

The first irrationality measure for $G$ was obtained by Jha \cite{Jha2026}, who proved
$\mu(G)\le10^8$. Carreira \cite{Carreira2026} studied general five-parameter determinant designs,
obtained $\mu(G)<2294$ for one of them, and formulated the best real-place constant of the method as
an equilibrium problem for two measures; in \cite{Carreira2026b} he records attempts to carry the
method to $\beta(4)$. Eskandari \cite{Eskandari2026} studies rational
approximations to $G$ from another point of view. For $L(2,\chi_{-3})$, Calegari, Dimitrov and Tang
proved linear independence of $1$, $\zeta(2)$ and $L(2,\chi_{-3})$ \cite{CDT2024}, and their
holonomy bounds give $\mu(L(2,\chi_{-3}))\le24781$ \cite[Thm.~5.1]{CDT2025}. A companion paper
\cite{Litsey2026b} proves measures for $G\pm5\pi^2/48$ and $L(2,\chi_{-3})$ with a related method.

\section{Overview of the method and of the new ideas}\label{sec:overview}

\subsection{From irrationality to a measure}
Write OpenAI's matrix as $\cA_N=\cA^0_N+G\,\cL_N$ with rational matrices $\cA^0_N$ and $\cL_N$, and put
$\Delta_N(X)=\det(\cA^0_N+X\cL_N)$, a polynomial in the constant. A symmetry of the rows of $\cL_N$ gives
a degree bound $\deg\Delta_N\le dN$, and multiplication by a common denominator and a factorial gives an
integer polynomial $Q_N$.

Two things are needed to turn OpenAI's argument into a measure. First, the hypothesis $G=a/b$ must be
replaced by a rational approximation: the determinant must be small not only at $X=G$ but at every $X$
within $e^{-\rho N}$ of $G$, for some constant $\rho$. Second, the nonvanishing at a rational point
$a/b$ must hold uniformly, along primes $p$ not dividing $b$, and the denominator bound must be uniform
in $a/b$. Given these, a standard argument (Proposition~\ref{prop:criterion}) gives $\mu(G)\le d\rho/\eta$
up to a factor coming from the choice of the prime, where $\eta N^2$ is the margin between the two
bounds. Making $\rho$ small and $\eta$ large is the content of the paper.

We use OpenAI's construction with different parameters, which gives a determinant of size $20N$ and
degree at most $18N$ (Section~\ref{sec:twenty}). Its denominators are computed exactly at every prime,
including the contribution of linear relations among the rows modulo $p$, and its nonvanishing is
proved by reduction modulo $p$ under the weaker hypothesis $p^2\nmid b$.

\subsection{New idea 1: a perturbation bound by quadrature}
To bound $\Delta_N(x)-\Delta_N(G)$ for $x$ near $G$, we write the matrix $\cL_N$ by an exact Gauss-type
quadrature formula as a combination of point evaluations of the row functions
(Lemma~\ref{lem:quadrature}). Expanding the determinant column by column gives mixed determinants in
which some columns are integrals and others are point values. An identity of Andr\'eief type with
fixed points (Lemma~\ref{lem:andreief}) writes each of them as an integral over fewer variables, and the
energy method bounds the integrand. The quadrature weights are bounded on a circle slightly larger than
the unit circle, with nodes chosen away from the point where the energy fields are largest. This gives a
perturbation exponent $\rho$ of the size of the constants in the energy estimate.

\subsection{New idea 2: divisibility of all coefficients}
The core of the arithmetic is that every coefficient of $Q_N$ is divisible by a power of each prime
$p$ in three intervals, $(52N/9,44N/7)$, $(8N,9N)$ and $(4N,9N/2)$, given by the rank deficiency of the
reduced matrix $p^2\cA^0_N\bmod p$ (Section~\ref{sec:content}). We prove these rank deficiencies with
identities for Chebyshev polynomials in characteristic $p$, such as $T_p=X^p$ and
$U_{p-1}=(X^2-1)^{(p-1)/2}$, and their consequences for the digits in base $p$ of the row polynomials.
By the prime number theorem the common factor has logarithm at least $0.649N^2$, which is added to
the margin $\eta$. This is in the spirit of the arithmetic method of Hata and Rhin--Viola, but the
common factor comes from linear algebra modulo $p$ rather than from a group of symmetries.

\subsection{New idea 3: prime squares in the denominator}
The polynomial $Q_N(X/q^2)$ is still integral at the primes $N<q\le2N$, so $q^{2j}$ divides the $j$-th
coefficient of $Q_N$. If $b$ is divisible by the squares of many such primes, the integer
$b^{\deg Q_N}Q_N(a/b)$ is divisible by their product. Choosing the \emph{largest} prime $p$ with
$p^2\nmid b$ in a suitable interval, every larger prime of the interval has its square dividing $b$, and
its contribution is recovered from the coefficients (Section~\ref{sec:proof}). This removes the loss
usually caused by the choice of the prime.

\subsection{New idea 4: trial potentials with complex poles}
The energy estimate needs explicit trial sequences, whose generating functions are potentials. The
extremal measure of the energy problem has a gap around $x=0$ in its support; near the edges of the
gap, and near $x=0$, Chebyshev expansions converge slowly. We add terms $\operatorname{Re}\,r\log(1-2xz+z^2)$,
which are logarithmic potentials of point charges at complex points close to these edges
(Section~\ref{sec:real}). The resulting fields are bounded above on their whole domains by a verified
computation on intervals, with an exact local Taylor expansion of the polynomial part.

\subsection{The deduction}
Combining these, the degree is $d=18$, the margin is $\eta=64169/25000$ and the perturbation exponent
is $\rho=259299/2500$. Since the choice of the prime costs nothing, the exponent is the limit
\[
 \mu(G)\le\frac{18\rho}{\eta}=\frac{46673820}{64169}=727.357\ldots,
\]
which gives Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
