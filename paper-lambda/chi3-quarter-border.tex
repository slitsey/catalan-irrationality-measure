% Quarter border and characteristic-zero selection.
\section{The number $L(2,\chi_{-3})$}
\label{sec:chi3-quarter}

For the frame $F_\chi$ we use a quarter border: the alternating geometry of parameter $c=\frac12$ at
the scales $r\equiv0\pmod4$, so that $4m=r$. We retain the full determinant together with its
principal restrictions obtained by omitting one common node. Their degree budget is still $r$.
The denominator and decay estimates below apply uniformly to every member.

\begin{theorem}[One common-node deletion]\label{thm:chi3-charzero-selection}
For every $r\ge1$, $m\ge0$ and rational input, the full conductor-three
determinant or one of its principal common-node deletions is nonzero.
No prime-band restriction on $m/r$ is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Here is the interpolation argument underlying the certified selector.
Write $N=r+2m$, $n=r+m$, and use the $r$ common nodes followed by the
alternating row and column nodes in the consecutive tail. After the fixed
normalization of the conductor-three frame, its divided differences have
prefix values $f_i$, with
\[
 f_{i+1}-f_i=\delta(i),\qquad
 \delta(z)=\frac{1-z}{216(z+1)(z+1/3)(z+1/2)}.
\]
The diagonal prefix $e_i$ satisfies
$e_{i+1}-e_i=-\delta'(i)$. Barycentric interpolation identifies either
kernel with pairs $(p,q)$ of degree less than $n$ satisfying
$q(i)=f_i p(i)$ at all $N$ nodes, and
$q'(c)=f_c p'(c)+(U-e_c)p(c)$ at the common nodes.

Two such pairs have zero cross product: its degree is at most $2n-2$,
while the value and first-jet conditions give $N+r=2n$ zeros counted
with multiplicity. If the kernel had dimension at least two, a nonzero
pair could therefore be chosen with both degrees at most $n-2$.
The discrete Wronskian
\begin{align*}
 W(z)={}&216(z+1)(z+1/3)(z+1/2)\\
 &\quad\cdot[q(z+1)p(z)-q(z)p(z+1)]\\
 &-(1-z)p(z)p(z+1).
\end{align*}
would have degree at most $2n-3$, but its prescribed values and first
jets supply $2n-2$ zeros. Thus $W=0$. This would make $q/p$ a rational
antidifference of $\delta$, which is impossible: the simple pole at $-1$
is isolated in its integer-translation class. After cancellation of a
numerator-denominator gcd, evaluating the cleared identity propagates
a denominator root indefinitely in one direction, contradicting finite
degree. The boundary case $n=1$ is immediate.

If the full determinant is nonzero, retain it. Otherwise both kernels are one dimensional. A nonzero kernel pair cannot vanish at every common node. Dividing by
their common node polynomial would give degree at most $m-1$ and the
same value interpolation at all $N$ nodes; its Wronskian would again
vanish by degree and root count. The left and right barycentric maps land in the same one-dimensional interpolation-pair space. At a common node their coordinates are the same $p(c)$ multiplied by nonzero punctured-node factors. Thus the same common index is nonzero on both sides, and their adjugate outer product has a nonzero diagonal entry there. That principal cofactor is the required deletion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The uniform arithmetic profile}
The singular indices of $F_\chi$ are those $k$ with $p\mid k(3k-2)(2k-1)$, and the relevant
classes of primes are modulo $6$. We first use assignment bounds with explicit potentials, as in
\S\ref{sssec:trop}: the residue-class potentials of (T1$'$), and potentials in which only row
potentials are raised, on nodes that have a congruent partner below the first pole. At a prime $p$
put $u=p/r$. We always use the first bound, and the smaller of the two when $r+2m\le2p$, the range
in which the second is proved. For the two classes of primes modulo $6$ this
gives the piecewise linear profiles $w_1,w_5$ below; at a common endpoint the two
neighbouring pieces agree.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{class}&\text{interval for }u&w(u)\\\hline
1,5 &[0,1/4]&5/2\\
1,5 &[1/4,1/2]&11/4-u\\
1 &[1/2,15/16]&9/4\\
1 &[15/16,9/8]&7/2-4u/3\\
1 &[9/8,3/2]&5-8u/3\\
1 &[3/2,2]&2-2u/3\\
1 &[2,3]&1-u/6\\
1 &[3,9/2]&3/2-u/3\\
5 &[1/2,5/6]&9/4\\
5 &[5/6,1]&7/2-3u/2\\
5 &[1,3/2]&4-2u\\
5 &[3/2,2]&5/2-u\\
5 &[2,3]&3/2-u/2
\end{array}
\]
Both profiles vanish beyond their last interval. Their integrals are $581/128$ and $355/96$,
and the prime number theorem in the two classes gives
\[
 \Lambda_0=\frac12\left(\frac{581}{128}+\frac{355}{96}\right)
         =\frac{3163}{768}.
\]
For finite $r$ the proof uses affine bounds with a fixed additive allowance for the rounding
of $m$ and of the residue endpoints; the contributions of prime powers and of the finitely
many excluded primes are kept, and they are $o(r^2)$. The entrywise denominator clears every
principal restriction, so this bound holds for the whole family with one deletion.

\paragraph{Small primes.}
For odd primes $p\le m$ the profile above ignores the bound
\[
 v_p(\operatorname{den})\le 2r+2m-\min(m\bmod p,\;p-m\bmod p),
\]
which holds at every level of the nodes and comes from the distance of $m$ to the nearest
multiple of $p$. On each interval $m/(n+1)<p<m/n$, $n\ge1$, it gives the additional saving
$\min\{m-np,(n+1)p-m\}$. The two affine pieces meet at $p=2m/(2n+1)$, and
\[
 \int_{m/(n+1)}^{m/n}\min\{m-np,(n+1)p-m\}\,dp
 =\frac{m^2}{2n(n+1)(2n+1)}.
\]
Using the intervals with $n\le16$, the prime number theorem in the two classes modulo $6$
gives the content constant
\[
 \Lambda_{16}=\Lambda_0-\frac1{16}
       \sum_{n=1}^{16}\frac1{2n(n+1)(2n+1)}
 =\frac{339258406689121}{82516315939200}.
\]
The finite-size argument is the same for every $n$, with a fixed additive rounding allowance.
The same denominator clears every principal restriction, so the family of determinants and
the selection of Theorem~\ref{thm:chi3-charzero-selection} are unchanged.

\paragraph{Windows and the rank bound.}
The window bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:window} (with $n=0$) and the rank bound of
\S\ref{sssec:rank} do not use the two-term structure: for a frame with several poles the bit $z$
of a fibre becomes the index of the arc between consecutive pole classes, and the arc below the first
pole still has a vanishing step function at the digit $0$. With the cuts $t\equiv0$, $t\equiv\frac23$
and $t\equiv\frac12$ modulo $p$, the windows lower the content by $853129/196035840$. The rank bound, in
the class $1\bmod 6$ on $\frac58\le u\le\frac56$ and in the class $5\bmod6$ on $\frac12\le u\le\frac58$, lowers
the integrals of the two classes by $\frac1{64}$ and $\frac1{320}$, that is, the content by $\frac3{320}$. Altogether,
\[
 \Lambda_\chi=\Lambda_{16}-\frac{853129}{196035840}-\frac3{320}=\frac{4733759984905769}{1155228423148800}=4.09768\ldots
\]
for the scales $r\equiv0\pmod4$.

\subsection{The analytic certificate}
The quarter border is the alternating geometry of parameter $c=\frac12$ at the scales $r\equiv0\pmod 4$, and the decay
estimate is Theorem~\ref{thm:decay} for the frame $F_\chi$, with $d=3$ digamma terms.
The node measure $\nu_{1/2}$ has mass $\frac54$ and energy
\[
 I(\nu_{1/2})=\frac{75}{32}-\frac98\log\frac32-\frac1{16}\log2 .
\]
A certificate measure on a polygon, with a cover by cells accepted by the checks of \S\ref{ssec:slope-cert}, gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
 D=\frac{4643511791526209475773}{10^{21}}=4.64351\ldots,\\ \gamma=\frac{37521870783566737}{5\cdot10^{15}}=7.50437\ldots
\end{gathered}
\]
Omitting one common node changes the energy only by a term linear in $r$, which is absorbed in the $o(r^2)$ error. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:chi3-charzero-selection} a retained determinant does not vanish at any rational point, so no prime has to
be chosen, and the deduction of Section~\ref{sec:proof} gives
\[
 \mu\bigl(L(2,\chi_{-3})\bigr)\le\frac\gamma{D-\Lambda_\chi}=\MuChiExact\ldots<\MuChi.
\]
