\title[Irrationality measures for differences of trigamma values]{Irrationality measures for differences of trigamma values}

\author{Sean Litsey}
\email{sean.litsey.math@gmail.com}

\date{October 29, 2026}

\subjclass[2020]{Primary 11J82; Secondary 11J72, 15A15, 31A15, 68V20}

\keywords{Catalan's constant, $L(2,\chi_{-3})$, irrationality exponent, L\"owner matrices,
Cauchy determinants, Andr\'eief identity, $p$-adic valuations, Hermite interpolation,
formal verification, Lean}

\begin{abstract}
For $0<s<t\le1$ let $\Psi(s,t)=\psi'(s)-\psi'(t)$, a difference of values of the trigamma function.
Catalan's constant is $G=\frac1{16}\Psi(\frac14,\frac34)$. Building on OpenAI's determinant method for
the irrationality of $G$, we prove irrationality measures for $\Psi(\frac kq,1)=\psi'(\frac kq)-\zeta(2)$
with $q\in\{3,4,5,7\}$ and $\gcd(k,q)=1$, and for $\Psi(\frac13,\frac23)=9L(2,\chi_{-3})$. In classical
terms, with $\mu$ the irrationality exponent,
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \mu\Bigl(G\pm\frac{5\pi^2}{48}\Bigr)\le \MuLamU,\qquad
 \mu\bigl(L(2,\chi_{-3})\bigr)\le \MuChi,\\
 \mu\Bigl(L(2,\chi_{-3})\pm\frac{\pi^2}9\Bigr)\le \MuThirdsU,\\
 \mu\bigl(\psi'(\tfrac k5)-\zeta(2)\bigr)\le\MuFifthsU\ (1\le k\le4),\\
 \mu\bigl(\psi'(\tfrac k7)-\zeta(2)\bigr)\le\MuSeventhsU\ (1\le k\le6).
\end{gathered}
\]
For $L(2,\chi_{-3})$ and $L(2,\chi_{-3})\pm\pi^2/9$, which Calegari, Dimitrov and Tang proved
irrational, the previous best bound we know of is their effective bound $\mu\le24781$. We know of no
earlier irrationality measure for the other numbers, and no earlier irrationality result for
$\psi'(\frac k5)-\zeta(2)$ and $\psi'(\frac k7)-\zeta(2)$.

The approximations come from determinants of \emph{rectangular} L\"owner matrices of digamma differences at integer
nodes, with a long common block and an interleaved border whose length is the only parameter of the geometry. The
denominators are bounded prime by prime by a finite list of local bounds (assignment problems, the rank of the
reduction modulo $p$, and windows of the border free of poles, where the reduction is translation invariant; for the
small primes, which meet the border several times, the windows are read modulo $p$), each an explicit integer expression in the
scale, the border, the prime and the first poles of the frame modulo $p$; one theorem turns a finite table of affine
pieces, checked by comparisons of rational numbers, into the content estimate for every shift with the same
denominator $q$. Nonvanishing follows from Hermite interpolation in characteristic zero, after deleting a bounded number
of common nodes. The size is bounded with Andr\'eief's identity and a lower bound for a discrete logarithmic energy,
obtained from a comparison measure and verified on a finite cover by cells.

The proof is formalized in Lean~4, with no unproved steps and only the standard axioms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

In October 2026 OpenAI \cite{OpenAI2026} proved that Catalan's constant
\[
 G=\sum_{j=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^j}{(2j+1)^2}=0.91596\,55941\ldots
\]
is irrational, and formalized the proof in Lean. (An earlier claimed proof
\cite{Sun2026} was found to contain a gap \cite{Wachs2026}.) Their argument, and the
irrationality-measure results built on it \cite{Jha2026,Carreira2026,Litsey2026}, use a
determinant of large size whose denominators and decay are balanced very closely.

In the present paper we study a different, much smaller, family of determinants: rectangular
L\"owner matrices of digamma differences. They approximate the differences of values of the trigamma
function $\psi'$,
\[
 \Psi(s,t)=\psi'(s)-\psi'(t)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\Bigl(\frac1{(n+s)^2}-\frac1{(n+t)^2}\Bigr),\qquad 0<s<t\le1 .
\]
Catalan's constant is one of these numbers, $G=\frac1{16}\Psi(\frac14,\frac34)$, and the companion paper
\cite{Litsey2026} treats it with OpenAI's construction. Here the second shift is $t=1$, with one exception,
$L(2,\chi_{-3})=\frac19\Psi(\frac13,\frac23)$, where
\[
 L(2,\chi_{-3})=\sum_{n\ge0}\Bigl(\frac1{(3n+1)^2}-\frac1{(3n+2)^2}\Bigr)=0.78130\,24128\ldots
\]
For $t=1$ and $s=\frac kq$ the value $\Psi(\frac kq,1)=\psi'(\frac kq)-\zeta(2)$ is an $L$-value or a
combination of $L$-values plus a rational multiple of $\pi^2$; for $q=4$ and $q=3$,
\begin{gather*}
 \Psi(\tfrac14,1)=8\Bigl(G+\frac{5\pi^2}{48}\Bigr),\qquad \Psi(\tfrac34,1)=-8\Bigl(G-\frac{5\pi^2}{48}\Bigr),\\
 \Psi(\tfrac13,1)=\frac92\Bigl(L(2,\chi_{-3})+\frac{\pi^2}9\Bigr),\qquad
 \Psi(\tfrac23,1)=-\frac92\Bigl(L(2,\chi_{-3})-\frac{\pi^2}9\Bigr).
\end{gather*}
We also write $\lambda_\pm=\frac G2\pm\frac{5\pi^2}{96}$, so that $G\pm5\pi^2/48=2\lambda_\pm$,
$\Psi(\frac14,1)=16\lambda_+$ and $\Psi(\frac34,1)=-16\lambda_-$.

Recall that the \emph{irrationality exponent} $\mu(\xi)$ of a real number $\xi$ is the
infimum of the $\mu$ such that $|\xi-a/b|>b^{-\mu}$ for all integers $a$ and all
sufficiently large $b$. It does not change under $\xi\mapsto r\xi+r'$ with $r,r'\in\Q$, $r\ne0$, so a
bound for a value of $\Psi$ is a bound for the classical constant in the same row of
Table~\ref{tab:main}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}\label{thm:thirds}\label{thm:fifths}
Let $(s,t)$ and $B$ be in one row of Table~\ref{tab:main}. For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $b_0$ such
that for all coprime integers $a$, $b$ with $b\ge b_0$,
\[
 \Bigl|\Psi(s,t)-\frac ab\Bigr|>b^{-B-\varepsilon}.
\]
In particular $\Psi(s,t)$ and the number in the same row are irrational, with irrationality exponent at
most $B$.
\end{theorem}

The rows are one construction with different shifts. The frame is the digamma difference
$\psi(z+t)-\psi(z+s)$, or for $L(2,\chi_{-3})$ a combination of three of them; for the pairs
$(\frac kq,1)$ the node geometry depends on $q$ only through one rational parameter. The shift
$s=\frac12$ also belongs to the family, with $\Psi(\frac12,1)=\psi'(\frac12)-\zeta(2)=2\zeta(2)=\pi^2/3$, and the
method applies to it, but the exponent it gives is larger than the known bounds for $\mu(\zeta(2))$
\cite{Giraudin26,LBKWSK26}, so we do not state it. Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, together with every theorem, proposition and
lemma used in its proof, has been verified in the Lean~4 proof assistant, with no unproved steps and only the standard
axioms; see Section~\ref{sec:lean}. The remarks and Section~\ref{sec:limits}, which discuss the method, are not part of the
formal proof. The formalization is available at \FormalizationLink. The use of AI
in this work is disclosed at the end of the paper.

\subsection{Previous results}
For earlier work on the arithmetic of $G$ and related values see
\cite{RivoalZudilin2003,Zudilin2003,Zudilin02Cheb,KrattRivoal08,KrattZudilin19} and the survey
\cite{Zudilin19Survey}; for recent rational approximations to $G$ see Eskandari \cite{Eskandari2026}. Finite
irrationality exponents for $G$ itself were obtained after OpenAI's proof by Jha \cite{Jha2026}, Carreira
\cite{Carreira2026}, Bojnal \cite{Bojnal2026}, Xu \cite{Xu2026} and the author \cite{Litsey2026}; Carreira \cite{Carreira2026b} also records attempts to carry the
determinant method to $\beta(4)$. Our searches found no previous irrationality result, and no irrationality measure, for $G-5\pi^2/48$ or $G+5\pi^2/48$.
Eskandari, Murty and Nemoto \cite{EMN2025} construct a $2$-dimensional mixed motive over $\Q$ with period $G$ and
use it to obtain linear forms in $1$ and $G$. Eskandari \cite{Eskandari2026} improves the approximation exponent of these
Rivoal--Zudilin type forms from $0.52$ to $0.62$ (a preprint; explicit approximations, not a lower bound for all
rationals). Sun's preprint \cite{Sun2026} claimed a proof of the irrationality of $G$; Wachs \cite{Wachs2026} reports that
its main estimate is not justified.

Calegari, Dimitrov and Tang proved that $1$, $\zeta(2)$ and $L(2,\chi_{-3})$ are linearly independent over
$\Q$ \cite{CDT2024}, by a new arithmetic holonomy bound applied to a construction of Zagier. In
\cite{CDT2025} they made holonomy bounds effective; Theorem~5.1 there gives $\mu(L(2,\chi_{-3}))\le24781$.
The remark following that theorem states that the same theorem and proof hold for any $\Q$-linear combination of
$L(2,\chi_{-3})$ and $\zeta(2)$; in particular $24781$ was the known bound for $L(2,\chi_{-3})\pm\frac23\zeta(2)$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} improves it to $\MuChi$ for $L(2,\chi_{-3})$, and to $\MuThirdsU$ for the
two numbers $L(2,\chi_{-3})\pm\frac23\zeta(2)$. Leibtag et al.\ \cite{LBKWSK26} find numerically that
Ap\'ery-like approximations from conservative matrix fields have positive approximation exponents for
$L(2,\chi_{-3})\pm\frac23\zeta(2)$, and a supplement to their paper uses one such field to give an Ap\'ery-style
proof of the theorem of Calegari, Dimitrov and Tang; the exponent it proves for each of these two numbers is only greater than $10^{-4}$.
For the numbers $\psi'(\frac k5)-\zeta(2)$ and $\psi'(\frac k7)-\zeta(2)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} our searches found no earlier irrationality result; the irrationality of $\mathrm{Li}_2(1/5)$ proved by Prellberg \cite{Prellberg26}
concerns a different number.

For comparison, among classical constants, the bound $\mu(\log2)<3.5746$ of Marcovecchio \cite{Marcovecchio09},
the bounds of Rhin and Viola for $\zeta(2)$ and $\zeta(3)$ \cite{RhinViola96,RhinViola01} (after Ap\'ery and Beukers
\cite{Apery79,Beukers79}) and Zudilin's bound for $\zeta(2)$ \cite{Zudilin14} have been joined, since September 2026, by a
series of new results, several of them
obtained with AI systems. OpenAI proved $\mu(\pi)=2$, with a Lean formalization \cite{OpenAIPi2026}, improving
the bound $\mu(\pi)<7.1033$ of Zeilberger and Zudilin \cite{ZeilbergerZudilin20}. Liu, Jiang and Zhang proved $\mu(\log r)=2$ for
every rational $r>0$, $r\ne1$, and for nonzero rational combinations of such logarithms \cite{LiuJiangZhang26}. Giraudin
lowered the bound for $\mu(\zeta(2))$ to $5.0193784$ with two hypergeometric constructions of Zudilin \cite{Zudilin14} at
new parameters \cite{Giraudin26}, and the bound for $\mu(\zeta(3))$ to $5.5138800$ by applying the Rhin--Viola method at a
new integral direction \cite{Giraudin26z3}. Leibtag, Barkan, Kalisch, Weinbaum, Shalyt and Kaminer
\cite{LBKWSK26} showed that the classical constructions are one-dimensional trajectories in conservative matrix
fields, and by searching over other trajectories obtained $\mu(\zeta(2))<4.62675$ and $\mu(\zeta(3))<5.4197$. Prellberg
\cite{Prellberg26} proved that $\mathrm{Li}_2(-1/4)$, $\mathrm{Li}_2(1/5)$ and $\mathrm{Li}_2(-1/3)$ are irrational, three
cases left open in \cite{CDT2024}, with the integrals of Rhin--Viola and Viola--Zudilin. Fauzan \cite{Fauzan2026} announced a proof that
$\zeta(5)$ is irrational, using rationally normalized Hankel determinants, and Lai
\cite{Lai2026} combined his method with simultaneous approximations of Zudilin and of Dauguet and Zudilin to show that
$1$, $\zeta(3)$ and $\zeta(4)$ are linearly independent over $\Q$.

\emph{Related work on determinants.} Irrationality proofs built on determinants of large size have a precedent
in Zudilin's determinantal criterion with Hankel determinants \cite{Zudilin}; the preprint of Liu, Jiang
and Zhang on logarithms \cite{LiuJiangZhang26} uses a separated-weight interpolation determinant, and the arguments of
Fauzan and Lai \cite{Fauzan2026,Lai2026} use Hankel determinants. Brown \cite{Brown26} proposes a multivariate
transfinite-diameter test for Mellin-type integrals, applied to $\zeta(2)$. The framework
we use, determinants whose decay is controlled by Cauchy determinants and Andr\'eief's identity and whose
denominators are controlled prime by prime, is OpenAI's \cite{OpenAI2026}. Our determinants are L\"owner
(divided-difference) matrices of a digamma difference, a matrix-valued analogue of Pad\'e and Hermite--Pad\'e
approximation \cite{NikishinSorokin91}. Formal proofs of irrationality results in Lean also include the
irrationality of $\zeta(3)$ by Liu, Zhang and Zhi \cite{LZZ25}.

\section{Overview of the method and of the new ideas}\label{sec:overview}

\subsection{L\"owner matrices of digamma differences}
For a function $F$ analytic near a finite set of distinct points, the \emph{L\"owner matrix}
\cite{Loewner34} is the matrix of divided differences, indexed by the points:
\[
 \Lw_F[t,t']=\frac{F(t')-F(t)}{t'-t}\ (t\ne t'),\qquad \Lw_F[t,t]=F'(t).
\]
It is a Cauchy-like structured matrix, and Hermite's formula gives
$\Lw_F=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint F(z)\,v(z)v(z)^{\top}dz$ with $v_t(z)=1/(z-t)$, so that a representation of
$F$ as a Stieltjes or contour integral exhibits $\Lw_F$ as a Gram matrix of Cauchy kernels.

For a rational shift $0<s<1$ with denominator $q$ we take the digamma difference $F_s$, and for $L(2,\chi_{-3})$ a
combination $F_\chi$ of three digamma functions, with integer nodes:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frames}
\begin{gathered}
 F_s(z)=\psi(z+1)-\psi(z+s),\qquad \omega=\tfrac1{q^2},\quad \xi_s=-\tfrac1{q^2}\Psi(s,1),\\
 F_{\chi}(z)=-\tfrac13\psi(z+\tfrac13)+\tfrac12\psi(z+\tfrac12)-\tfrac16\psi(z+1),\\
 \omega=-\tfrac16,\quad \xi_\chi=\tfrac14L(2,\chi_{-3}).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In each case $\xi=\omega\,F'(0)$, while $F(t')-F(t)$ and $F'(t)-F'(0)$ are rational for integers $t,t'\ge0$. Hence, for
any finite sets of nonnegative integer nodes $R$ (rows) and $Q$ (columns),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
 \omega\,\Lw_F[R,Q]=M[R,Q]+\xi\,E_{R,Q},\qquad E_{R,Q}[t,t']=\mathbf 1[t=t'],
\end{equation}
with an explicit rational symmetric kernel $M$ on $\Z_{\ge0}^2$ (\S\ref{sec:construction}). Replacing $\xi$ by
a variable $x$ gives a matrix polynomial $\Phi(x)=M[R,Q]+xE_{R,Q}$, whose determinant $P(x)$ is a polynomial
with rational coefficients of degree at most $|R\cap Q|$, and $P(\xi)=\det(\omega\,\Lw_F[R,Q])$. If
$|\xi-a/b|$ is very small, $P(a/b)$ is very close to $P(\xi)$, which is tiny; on the other hand
$b^{|R\cap Q|}\den(P)\,P(a/b)$ is an integer. If it is not zero we get a contradiction as soon as
\[
 \underbrace{\log\den P}_{\text{content}}+|R\cap Q|\log b\ <\ \underbrace{-\log|P(a/b)|}_{\text{decay}} .
\]
Three estimates are therefore needed: an upper bound for the \emph{content} $\log\den P$, an upper bound
for $|P(x)|$ near $\xi$ (the \emph{decay}), and \emph{nonvanishing} of $P(a/b)$ for some member of a family of
determinants of a size that can be chosen in terms of $b$. Each estimate is proved once, for every shift, and the
families differ only in a few parameters.

\subsection{New idea 1: rectangular frames with an interleaved border}
We take a long common block $C=[0,r)$, which carries the variable $x$, and a border of $m\approx\alpha r$ row nodes and
$m$ column nodes, interleaved:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 X'=\{r,r+2,\dots,r+2m-2\}\ \text{(rows only)},\\ Y'=\{r+1,r+3,\dots,r+2m-1\}\ \text{(columns only)},
\end{gathered}
\]
so that $R=C\sqcup X'$ and $Q=C\sqcup Y'$. The determinant $P(x)=\det\Phi(x)$ still has degree at most $r$, so the
integrality loss $|R\cap Q|\log q$ is that of $r$ nodes, but the matrix has size $r+m$ and its decay is that of a
configuration of $(1+\alpha)r$ nodes. The content grows too, but less: the border rows and columns never meet the
diagonal. The \emph{members} of the family are the principal restrictions obtained by deleting a bounded number of
common nodes. The single parameter of the geometry is $c=2\alpha$, the length of the border relative to $r$; we use
$c=\frac15,\frac3{10},\frac29,\frac16$ for $q=4,3,5,7$ and $c=\frac12$ for $L(2,\chi_{-3})$. The interleaving of $X'$
and $Y'$ is essential: it keeps the two Cauchy determinants in Andr\'eief's formula comparable (\S\ref{sec:decay}),
and it gives the reduction of the matrix modulo a prime the alternating structure on which several of the arithmetic
bounds of \S\ref{sec:content} rest.

\subsection{New idea 2: the content from a finite list of local bounds}
As in the work of Rhin and Viola \cite{RhinViola96,RhinViola01}, we bound the $p$-adic valuation of
$\den P$ prime by prime and sum the bounds against $\log p$ with the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions.
For a prime $p$ in the range $p\asymp r$ the relevant data are the scale $r$, the border size $m$, the prime, and the
first index $k_p\ge1$ at which the second digamma term of $F_s$ has a pole modulo $p$; we have $k_p=\kappa p+O(1)$
with $\kappa=j/q$, where $j\equiv qs\,p^{-1}\pmod q$ is the \emph{root class} of $p$. The frame of $L(2,\chi_{-3})$ has two
such first poles, and on each class of primes modulo $6$ both are affine functions of one index of the same kind
(Section~\ref{sec:chi3-quarter}).

We prove a finite list of local bounds, each by its own mechanism: the assignment (tropical) bound with explicit
potentials, three bounds for the rank of the reduction modulo $p$ of a rescaled matrix, and a bound from windows of
consecutive border nodes free of poles, in which the reduction is translation invariant between residue classes. The
window bound also applies to the primes $p\le m$, which meet the border several times: reduced modulo $p$, the border
covers an arc of residue classes once more than the others, and on this arc the translation argument improves the
assignment bound.
Every one of them is an explicit integer expression in $(r,m,p,k_p)$, built from affine forms with $\min$, $\max$,
truncated subtraction, the distance to $p\Z$ and the multiple $p\lfloor r/p\rfloor$, and holds in a range of $p/r$
described by linear inequalities.
Replacing $(r,m,p,k_p)$ by $(1,\alpha,\theta,\kappa\theta)$ turns each bound into a piecewise affine profile in
$\theta=p/r$, up to an error bounded by an explicit constant. The content constant is then
\[
 \Lambda=\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_j\int_0^\infty\mathcal P_j(\theta)\,d\theta,
\]
where $\mathcal P_j$ is the pointwise minimum of the valid profiles for the root class $j$. A finite table of affine
pieces above $\mathcal P_j$, together with the name of the bound that justifies each piece, is a certificate for $\Lambda$:
whether it is correct is decided by finitely many comparisons of rational numbers. One theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:content}) turns an accepted table into the content estimate. The bounds depend on the shift $s$ only
through the root class, so the same table serves every shift with denominator $q$, and the numbers $\psi'(s)-\zeta(2)$ for
these shifts receive the same exponent.

\subsection{New idea 3: nonvanishing by Hermite interpolation in characteristic zero}
The kernel of $\Phi(x_0)$ at a rational point $x_0$ is identified with a space of pairs of polynomials satisfying
Hermite interpolation conditions at the nodes. Two such pairs have vanishing cross product by a count of degrees and
zeros, and a further pair in the kernel would give a rational antidifference of the increment of the frame, which
does not exist because the pole at $-1$ is isolated in its class modulo $\Z$. This bounds the corank of $\Phi(x_0)$ by a
number depending only on the frame and the period of the geometry, and a principal restriction deleting at most that
many common nodes is nonsingular (Theorem~\ref{thm:selection}). No prime enters, so the size $r$ can be chosen freely
in terms of $b$.

\subsection{New idea 4: decay through a certificate measure checked on a finite cover}
Moving Hermite's contour to a polygonal path $\Gamma$ in the left half-plane writes $\omega\,\Lw_F[R,Q]$ as a Gram matrix
$\int v_tv_{t'}\,dm$ of a complex measure $m$ (signed atoms at poles of $F$ plus an exponentially small density on
$\Gamma$). Andr\'eief's identity with two families of functions (rows and columns) gives
\[
 |\det\Lw_F[R,Q]|\le\frac1{n!}\int\bigl|\mathsf C_R(z)\bigr|\,\bigl|\mathsf C_Q(z)\bigr|\prod_{i=1}^nd|m|(z_i),
\]
where $\mathsf C_R,\mathsf C_Q$ are Cauchy determinants. Taking logarithms turns the integrand into a discrete
logarithmic energy of the free points $z_i$ against the nodes in an external field. We bound this energy from below by
comparing it with a \emph{certificate measure} $\sigma$, a density that is polynomial on each edge of finitely many
segments, through the conditional positive definiteness of the logarithmic kernel. What is left is a lower bound for
one explicit function $g$ on the contour, on the segment carrying the atoms and on the interval of the nodes. We prove
it on a finite cover of these sets by cells, each checked in exact rational arithmetic. One theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:decay}) gives the decay for every geometry parameter $c\le1$ and every frame whose poles are separated
from the contour; a family supplies only $\sigma$, the polygon and the cover.

\subsection{The deduction}
Write $\eta=D-\Lambda$ and let $\gamma$ be the window constant of the decay estimate: the decay holds at every point
within $e^{-\gamma r}$ of $\xi$. The three estimates are combined in \S\ref{sec:proof}; the result is
\[
 \mu(\xi)\le\frac\gamma\eta=\frac\gamma{D-\Lambda}.
\]
Table~\ref{tab:data} lists the constants for every row of Table~\ref{tab:main}.

\subsection{Formal verification}

\section{The construction}\label{sec:construction}

\subsection{The kernels}
Let $0<s<1$ be rational with denominator $q$, and put $o=q(1-s)$, so that $k-1+s=(qk-o)/q$. For the function $F_s$ of
\eqref{eq:frames} the kernel $M$ of \eqref{eq:shift} is as follows; here $0\le t<t'$ are integers and $M[t',t]=M[t,t']$:
\begin{align*}
 M[t,t']&=\frac1{q^2(t'-t)}\sum_{k=t+1}^{t'}\Bigl(\frac1k-\frac q{qk-o}\Bigr),\\
 M[t,t]&=-\frac1{q^2}\sum_{k=1}^{t}\Bigl(\frac1{k^2}-\frac{q^2}{(qk-o)^2}\Bigr).
\end{align*}
For $s=\frac34$ this is $M[t,t']=-\frac1{t'-t}\sum_{k=t+1}^{t'}\frac1{16k(4k-1)}$. For the function $F_\chi$,
\[
 M[t,t']=\frac1{6(t'-t)}\sum_{k=t+1}^{t'}g(k),\qquad M[t,t]=\frac16\sum_{k=1}^{t}g'(k),
\]
with $g(k)=\frac1{3k-2}-\frac1{2k-1}+\frac1{6k}$, so that
$-\frac16g'(k)=\frac1{2(3k-2)^2}-\frac1{3(2k-1)^2}+\frac1{36k^2}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:loewner}
For each function of \eqref{eq:frames}, $\omega\,\Lw_F[R,Q]=M[R,Q]+\xi E_{R,Q}$ for all finite sets
$R,Q\subset\Z_{\ge0}$, with $\omega$ and $\xi$ as in \eqref{eq:frames}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
From $\psi(w+1)-\psi(w)=1/w$ one gets $F_s(k)-F_s(k-1)=\frac1k-\frac1{k-1+s}=\frac1k-\frac q{qk-o}$, and
telescoping gives the off-diagonal entries. Similarly $\psi'(w+1)-\psi'(w)=-1/w^2$ gives $F_s'(t)-F_s'(0)$. Finally
$\omega F_s'(0)=q^{-2}(\psi'(1)-\psi'(s))=-q^{-2}\Psi(s,1)=\xi_s$. For $F_\chi$ use $\psi'(\frac12)=\frac{\pi^2}2$ and
$\psi'(\frac13)=\frac{2\pi^2}3+\frac92L(2,\chi_{-3})$, which give $-\frac16F_\chi'(0)=-\frac16\sum_{k\ge1}g'(k)=\frac14L(2,\chi_{-3})$.
\end{proof}

Since $\mu(u\xi+u')=\mu(\xi)$ for rational $u\ne0$ and $u'$, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a statement about the numbers
$\xi$ of \eqref{eq:frames}.

\subsection{Geometries}
\begin{definition}\label{def:geometry}
Let $r,m\ge0$ and $e\in\{0,1\}$. The \emph{geometry} $\mathcal G(r,m,e)$ consists of the common nodes
$C=[0,r)\cup\{r+2m:\ e=1\}$, the row nodes $X'=\{r+2i:0\le i<m\}$ and the column nodes
$Y'=\{r+2i+1:0\le i<m\}$. Rows are indexed by $R=C\sqcup X'$, columns by $Q=C\sqcup Y'$. For a set $I\subseteq C$ of
deleted common nodes, the matrix
\[
 \Phi_{\mathcal G,I}(x)=\bigl(M[t,t']+x\,\mathbf 1[t=t'\in C\setminus I]\bigr)_{t\in R\setminus I,\ t'\in Q\setminus I}
\]
is square of size $|C\setminus I|+m$; put $P_{\mathcal G,I}(x)=\det\Phi_{\mathcal G,I}(x)\in\Q[x]$, and write
$\Phi_{\mathcal G}$, $P_{\mathcal G}$ when $I=\emptyset$.
\end{definition}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:loewner}, $\Phi_{\mathcal G}(\xi+h)=\omega\,\Lw_F[R,Q]+h\,E_{R,Q}$ for real $h$. The geometry
with $m=0$, $e=0$ is the square L\"owner matrix of size $r$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:altgeom}
Let $0<c\le1$ and $\alpha=c/2$. The \emph{alternating geometry} of parameter $c$ at an even scale $r$ is
$\mathcal G(r,m(r),0)$ with $m(r)=\lceil\alpha r-\frac12\rceil$, so that $|m(r)-\alpha r|\le\frac12$ and the border
$[r,r+2m)$ has length $cr+O(1)$. For a depth $K\ge0$, its \emph{members} are the matrices $\Phi_{r,I}=\Phi_{\mathcal G,I}$
with $|I|\le K$, and $P_{r,I}=\det\Phi_{r,I}$.
\end{definition}

For the frames $F_s$ we use $c=\frac15,\frac3{10},\frac29,\frac16$ for $q=4,3,5,7$, and for $F_\chi$ we use $c=\frac12$
(Section~\ref{sec:chi3-quarter}). All $o(1)$ terms below are uniform over the members as $r\to\infty$.

\begin{lemma}[integrality]\label{lem:integrality}
$P_{\mathcal G,I}$ has degree at most $|C\setminus I|$. If $a/b$ is a fraction
in lowest terms with $P_{\mathcal G,I}(a/b)\ne0$, then $|\den(P_{\mathcal G,I})\,P_{\mathcal G,I}(a/b)|\ge b^{-|C\setminus I|}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The variable occurs only in the $|C\setminus I|$ diagonal entries indexed by $C\setminus I$, so each Leibniz term has
degree at most $|C\setminus I|$. Then $b^{|C\setminus I|}\den(P_{\mathcal G,I})\,P_{\mathcal G,I}(a/b)$ is a nonzero integer.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
When the $m\times m$ block $M[X',Y']$ is invertible, the Schur complement gives
$P_{\mathcal G}(x)=\det M[X',Y']\cdot\det(xI_C+\widetilde M)$ with
$\widetilde M=M[C,C]-M[C,Y']M[X',Y']^{-1}M[X',C]$. Thus the rectangular determinant is, up to a constant, the
characteristic polynomial of a rational matrix of size $|C|$, as in the square case. We do not need this
factorisation: Lemma~\ref{lem:integrality} only uses the degree bound.
\end{remark}

\section{Arithmetic: the content}\label{sec:content}

In this section we bound the common denominator of the coefficients of the members $P_{r,I}$ for the frames $F_s$.
The method is that of Rhin and Viola \cite{RhinViola96,RhinViola01}: the $p$-adic valuation of the
denominator is bounded prime by prime, and the bounds are summed against $\log p$ with the prime number theorem in
arithmetic progressions. What is new is the form of the local bounds. Each of them is proved by its own argument, but
all of them are explicit integer expressions in four integers attached to the prime, and their minimum, integrated,
gives the content constant. The comparison between the local bounds and a table of affine pieces is a finite
computation with rational numbers, and one theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:content}) turns a table that passes it into the
content estimate. The case of $L(2,\chi_{-3})$ is treated in Section~\ref{sec:chi3-quarter}.

\subsection{The root class of a prime}
Fix the frame $F_s$, with $s$ of denominator $q$, and the alternating geometry of parameter $c=2\alpha$. Put $N=r+2m$. For
a prime $p>q$ the indices $k\ge1$ at which the entries of $M$ acquire a denominator divisible by $p$ are the multiples of
$p$ and the solutions of $qk\equiv o\pmod p$. Let $k=k_p$ be the least of the latter; then
\[
 qk_p=jp+o,\qquad 0<j<q,\qquad j\equiv qs\,p^{-1}\pmod q .
\]
We call $j$ the \emph{root class} of $p$ and put $\kappa=\kappa_j=j/q$. Then $k_p-\kappa p=o/q\in[\frac1q,1-\frac1q]$:
the first pole is at distance less than one from $\kappa p$, uniformly in $s$. Throughout,
$q(r+2m)<p^2$, so that every pole below $N$ is simple and only the first power of $p$ matters; the primes with
$p^2\le q(r+2m)$ and the prime powers contribute $O(r^{3/2}\log r)$ in total.

\subsection{Gauss valuations, the assignment bound and the rank of the reduction}
Fix a prime $p$. Let $\cO\subset\Q(x)$ be the localisation of $\Z_{(p)}[x]$ at the prime ideal $(p)$; it is a
discrete valuation ring with uniformiser $p$ and residue field $\F_p(x)$, and its valuation $v$ restricted to
$\Q[x]$ is the \emph{Gauss valuation} $v(f)=\min_k v_p(f_k)$, $f=\sum f_kx^k$. By Gauss's lemma $v(fg)=v(f)+v(g)$,
and $v_p(\den f)=\max(0,-v(f))$. For a member we write $e_p(r,I)=v_p(\den P_{r,I})$.

\begin{lemma}[assignment bound and LP duality]\label{lem:trop}
Let $B$ be a square matrix over $\Q[x]$ with rows $I$ and columns $J$, let $W_{tt'}=\max(0,-v(B_{tt'}))$, and let
$\tau(W)$ be the largest weight $\sum W_{tt'}$ of a perfect matching between $I$ and $J$. Then $v(\det B)\ge-\tau(W)$. If
real numbers $u_t$, $v_{t'}$ satisfy $W_{tt'}\le u_t+v_{t'}$ for all $t\in I$ and $t'\in J$, then
$\tau(W)\le\sum_tu_t+\sum_{t'}v_{t'}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each Leibniz term is a product of entries along a perfect matching, so its valuation is at least minus the weight of the
matching, and $v$ of a sum is at least the minimum. The second statement is weak LP duality for the assignment problem:
the weight of a perfect matching is at most $\sum_tu_t+\sum_{t'}v_{t'}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[rank of the reduction]\label{lem:rank}
Let $(u,v)$ be integer potentials with $W_{tt'}\le u_t+v_{t'}$ for the matrix $\Phi_{r,I}$ at $p$, of value
$\tau'=\sum_tu_t+\sum_{t'}v_{t'}$. Then $B=\operatorname{diag}(p^{u})\,\Phi_{r,I}\,\operatorname{diag}(p^{v})$ has entries in
$\cO$, and
\[
 e_p(r,I)\le\tau'-\corank_{\F_p(x)}(B\bmod p).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$v(\det\Phi_{r,I})=-\tau'+v(\det B)$, and over the discrete valuation ring $\cO$ the Smith normal form shows
$v(\det B)\ge\corank(B\bmod p)$.
\end{proof}

The rank bounds below use a refinement of Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} at second order. Suppose that after a unimodular change
of rows and columns over $\cO$ the first $n_1$ rows and the first $n_2$ columns of $B$ are divisible by $p$, and the
$n_1\times n_2$ block where they meet is divisible by $p^2$. Dividing the first $n_1$ rows by $p$ leaves the first $n_2$
columns divisible by $p$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corner}
 v(\det B)\ge n_1+n_2 .
\end{equation}
The vectors that produce such rows and columns are kernel vectors of the reduction of $B$ on single residue classes,
and the condition on the corner is a statement about their pairing at first order, which we now describe. Let
$B=p^2\Phi_{r,I}$ (all potentials equal to $1$). Since $q(r+2m)<p^2$, two distinct nodes $t\ne t'$ have
$v_p(t'-t)\ge1$ only if $t\equiv t'\pmod p$, and then $v_p(t'-t)=1$. Hence $B$ has entries in $\cO$, its reduction is
block diagonal over the residue classes modulo $p$ (the \emph{fibres}), and for nodes $t$, $t'$ in distinct fibres
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstorder}
 B_{tt'}\equiv\frac{p\,(V(t')-V(t))}{t'-t}\pmod{p^2},
\end{equation}
where $V(t)\in\F_p$ is the residue of $p$ times the partial sum $\sum_{k\le t}(\frac1k-\frac q{qk-o})$, up to the
normalising factor $q^{-2}$; $V$ is a step function of $t$ which jumps only at the poles $t\equiv0$ and $t\equiv k_p$. Since
$t'-t$ is prime to $p$, only its residue matters in \eqref{eq:firstorder}.

For a vector $\mathbf y$ indexed by nodes, its \emph{moment pair} is
\[
 \hat{\mathbf y}=\Bigl(\sum_ty_t,\ \sum_ty_tV(t)\Bigr).
\]

\begin{lemma}[pairing]\label{lem:pairing}
Let $\mathbf y$ be supported on the rows of one fibre and $\mathbf y'$ on the columns of a different fibre, and let $t$ and
$t'$ be nodes of the first and of the second fibre. Then
\[
 \mathbf y^{\top}B\,\mathbf y'\equiv\frac p{t'-t}\,\det\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\mathbf y}\\ \hat{\mathbf y}'\end{pmatrix}\pmod{p^2}.
\]
In particular the pairing vanishes modulo $p^2$ whenever the moment pairs of $\mathbf y$ and $\mathbf y'$ are proportional.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand $\mathbf y^{\top}B\,\mathbf y'$ with \eqref{eq:firstorder}.
\end{proof}

The moment pairs are never both zero, by the displacement structure of L\"owner matrices.

\begin{lemma}[moment lemma]\label{lem:moment}
Let $A$ be a matrix with rows indexed by a set $R_0$ of distinct integers and columns by a set $C_0$, $|R_0|=|C_0|+1$,
whose off-diagonal entries are $A(t,t')=(L(t')-L(t))/(t'-t)$ for some function $L$, and whose diagonal entries (on
$R_0\cap C_0$) are arbitrary. Then $A$ has a nonzero left kernel vector $\mathbf y$ with
$(\sum_ty_t,\sum_ty_tL(t))\ne(0,0)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
With $D_R$, $D_C$ the diagonal matrices of the indices, $D_RA-AD_C=L_R\mathbf 1^{\top}-\mathbf 1L_C^{\top}$ (the diagonal
entries cancel on common indices). The left kernel $\mathcal N$ is nonzero. If every $\mathbf y\in\mathcal N$ had zero
moment pair, multiplying on the left by $\mathbf y^{\top}$ would give $\mathbf y^{\top}D_RA=0$, so $D_R$ would preserve
$\mathcal N$; as $D_R$ is diagonal with distinct entries, $\mathcal N$ would contain a standard basis vector, supported on
one index $t$, whose moment pair is $(1,L(t))\ne(0,0)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The local bounds}
We now list the local bounds. Each is a statement of the following form: for every member $P_{r,I}$ with $|I|\le K$,
every prime $p>p_0$ with $q(r+2m)<p^2$ and every $p$ satisfying the stated conditions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localform}
 e_p(r,I)\le\tau(r,m,p,k_p)+\mathrm{debit}\cdot|I| ,
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is the stated expression and the \emph{debit}, $0$ or $1$, is the stated cost of each deleted node. Here $p_0$
depends only on the frame. We write $(\cdot)_+=\max(\cdot,0)$, $\|m\|_p$ for the distance of $m$ to $p\Z$, and $u_0=(k_p+p-r)_+$.

\begin{theorem}[local bounds]\label{thm:local}
The following bounds of the form \eqref{eq:localform} hold.

\subsubsection{The assignment bounds (T1)--(T5)}\label{sssec:trop}
These follow from Lemma~\ref{lem:trop} with explicit potentials. By the ultrametric inequality, an off-diagonal entry
$M[t,t']$ has $-v_p\le1$ if the interval $(t,t']$ contains a pole, plus $1$ if $p\mid t'-t$, and $0$ otherwise; a diagonal
entry $M[t,t]$ has $-v_p\le2$ if $t\ge k_p$ and $0$ if $t<k_p$. (T1) is the trivial bound with potentials $1$ on every
row and column. For (T1$'$) the potentials are $\frac12,\frac32,1$ on rows and $\frac32,\frac12,1$ on columns of a residue
class modulo $p$, according as $X'$ has more, fewer or as many nodes in the class as $Y'$; since the border alternates
between $X'$ and $Y'$, the imbalance is at most one per class and the number of unbalanced classes is $2\|m\|_p$. For
(T2)--(T5) one starts from the potential $\mathbf 1[t\ge k_p]$ on the common nodes $t$, which pays for the diagonal, and adds
$1$, or $1+\mathbf 1[z\ge p]$, on the border nodes $z$, raising the potentials of the common nodes that have a congruent
partner in the border. In each range one checks $W\le u+v$ entrywise, using two facts: two distinct nodes not
congruent modulo $p$ have weight at most $1$, and two border nodes are never congruent once $2m<p$. For (T5), with
$N\le p$ and $r\le k_p$, no common node pays for the diagonal and only the border nodes beyond $k_p$ carry potential
$1$; in particular (T5) vanishes once $k_p\ge N$, which happens for $p/r>\max(1+2\alpha,1/\kappa)+o(1)$. All cases are
checked in the formal proof.

\subsubsection{Primes near $r$: the bounds (LP1) and (LP2)}\label{sssec:lp}
For $p$ close to $r$ the reduction of the rescaled matrix has a large block of rank one per residue class, and the
corank can be computed. We give the proof of (LP2). The bound (LP1), for the primes with $p\le r<p+k_p$ and $N\le2p$, is
proved by the same method: with the potentials of (T2), the elimination of an invertible block of common nodes brings the
reduction to the form $\begin{psmallmatrix}\tilde xI&G_Y\\ G_X&0\end{psmallmatrix}$, with $\tilde x$ the variable $x$ plus a
constant, in which the border nodes below $p+k_p$
contribute rows and columns proportional to unit vectors, and the Frobenius--K\"onig theorem bounds its rank. The saving over
(T2) is $(\min(2m,p+k_p-r)-m)_+$, where $\min(2m,p+k_p-r)$ is the number of border nodes below $p+k_p$; we refer to the
formal proof for the details.
% Lean: Content.Smith.LP1Hyp.gle; branch Content.BankShared.TwoTerm.lp1_gle_tt.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lp2}
Assume $r<p<r+m$, $k=k_p\le r$ and $N=r+2m\le2p$, and that every pole below $N$ is simple. Then
$v(\det\Phi_{r,\emptyset})\ge-(2(r-k)+3m)$. The same holds for every member, with debit $1$ per deleted node.
\end{theorem}
% Lean: Content.BankShared.TwoTerm.LP2HypW.gle; branch lp2w_gle_tt (catalogue entry brLP2w).
\begin{proof}
Use the potentials $u(t)=\mathbf 1[k\le t]$ for common nodes $t<r$ and $u(t)=1+\mathbf 1[p\le t]$ for border nodes $t$, of value
$\tau'=2(r-k)+2m+(N-p)$. Since $N\le2p$, the poles below $N$ are $k$, $p$, and $p+k$ if $p+k<N$; each is simple, and the
residue of $p$ times the increment $\frac1t-\frac q{qt-o}$ at them is $-q/j$, $1$ and $-q/(q+j)$ respectively.

\emph{Rows and columns beyond $p+k$ vanish.} Let $z\in[p+k,N)$ be a border node, so $u(z)=2$, and let $t$ be a node of
the other kind. If $t<k$, then $u(t)=0$, the partial sum over $(t,z]$ has valuation at least $-1$ and $z-t\in(p,2p)$
is prime to $p$; otherwise $u(t)\ge1$ and $v_p(M[t,z])\ge-2$. In both cases the rescaled entry is divisible by $p$.

\emph{The remaining entries.} For nodes below $p+k$ the only poles are $k$ and $p$. With $B_0=[0,k)$ and $B_1=[k,r)$
the reduction has the following blocks, with $\omega=q^{-2}$ the normalisation of \eqref{eq:frames}: on $B_0\times B_0$ the
regular part plus $x$ on the diagonal; on $B_0\times B_1$ the entries $-\omega q/(j(t-t'))$ ($t'\in B_0$, $t\in B_1$); on $B_1\times B_1$ the scalar
matrix $\omega(q/j)^2I$; a border node $z\in[r,p)$ against $t'\in B_0$ gives $\omega q/(j(z-t'))$; a border node
$z\in[p,p+k)$ against $t'\in B_0$ gives $\omega(1-q/j)$ if $t'=z-p$ and $0$ otherwise; all other border entries vanish.

\emph{Rank.} Delete the zero rows and columns found above; this does not change the rank. Eliminating the scalar block
on $B_1$, the Schur complement on $B_0$ is $\tilde xI$ plus a constant multiple of the matrix with entries
$\sum_{i=1}^{p-r}\frac1{(t+i)(t'+i)}$ ($t,t'\in B_0$), where $\tilde x$ is $x$ plus a constant, by the reflection
$H_{p-1-n}\equiv H_n\pmod p$ of harmonic numbers. The border nodes $z\in[r,p)$, indexed by $i=p-z$, are all present because
$N>p$; even offsets are rows and odd offsets are columns, and together they cover $1\le i\le p-r$, so row and column
operations remove that multiple and leave
$\begin{psmallmatrix}\tilde xI&G_Y\\ G_X&0\end{psmallmatrix}$. A Schur complement on $\tilde xI$ (here it is essential that $x$
is an indeterminate, so that $\tilde x$ is a unit) shows that its rank is $k+\rank(G_XG_Y)$. In $G_XG_Y$ the block between the high
rows and the high columns is zero, since their offsets $z-p$ have opposite parities, so $\rank(G_XG_Y)\le p-r$. In
total the rank of the reduction is at most $(r-k)+k+(p-r)=p$, the corank is at least $r+m-p$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} gives $v(\det)\ge(r+m-p)-\tau'=-(2(r-k)+3m)$. Deleting a common node changes the potentials by at
most one.
\end{proof}

Under the hypothesis $N\le p+k$ the third pole does not occur and the proof is shorter; the observation that the border
nodes beyond $p+k$ only contribute rows and columns that vanish modulo $p$ allows the whole range $N\le2p$, which on
the interval $r<p<r+m$ is automatic.

\subsubsection{Pole-free windows: the bound (W)}\label{sssec:window}
Like the rank bounds, the bound (W) does not come from the valuations of the entries alone. Between two consecutive poles
the step function $V$ of \eqref{eq:firstorder} is constant, and the reductions of the fibres of nearby residue classes
are then translates of one another. Kernel vectors can be transported from one fibre to the next, and their pairings vanish
modulo $p^2$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:pairing}; the corner bound \eqref{eq:corner} turns this into a saving at second order.

A \emph{cut} is an integer $t$ with $t\equiv0$, $t\equiv k_p$ or $t\equiv r\pmod p$. A window $[t_1,t_2)$ of integers is
\emph{cut-free} if no cut lies in the open interval $(t_1,t_2)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:window}
Assume that every pole below $N$ is simple and $N<p^2$. Let $n\ge0$ with $np\le m$, put $N_n=r+2(m-np)$, and let
$[t_1,t_2)$ be a cut-free window with $\max(r,N_n-p)\le t_1\le t_2\le\min(N_n,r+p)$. Then every member satisfies
\[
 e_p(r,I)\le2r+2m-(t_2-t_1).
\]
\end{theorem}
% Lean: Content.BankShared.TwoTerm.Pre.prefix_gle (any frame whose poles below N are simple); branches brWin (n = 0,
% Check/Catalogue.lean) and brPre (Check/Prefix.lean).
For $n=0$ the windows lie in the border above $\max(r,N-p)$, and the bound applies to primes $p>m$. For $n=\lfloor m/p\rfloor\ge1$
it applies to the small primes $p\le m$, where among the assignment bounds only (T1) and (T1$'$) are available. Read
modulo $p$, the window range is then the arc of residues that receive an odd number of border nodes, and the saving over
(T1$'$) is the amount, if any, by which the length of the longest cut-free run of this arc exceeds $\|m\|_p$. For other $n$
the window range is empty. The proof
uses the frame only through its poles: for a frame with several digamma terms the same statement holds when the cuts are
the residues of all the poles and of $r$.

\begin{proof}
Let $B=p^2\Phi$, with independent diagonal variables on the common nodes (every member is a coefficient of this
determinant, so a bound for it bounds every member). Let $i<i'$ be two positions of the window; they lie in different
residue classes. Translation $t\mapsto t+(i'-i)$ maps the nodes of the fibre of $i$ bijectively onto those of the fibre of
$i'$: common nodes to common nodes, since no $t\equiv r$ lies in $(i,i']$; border nodes to border nodes, since both classes
lie in the window range; and $X'$ to $Y'$ when $i'-i$ is odd. Since there is no pole in $(i,i']$, the step function $V$
and the diagonal residues are unchanged, and so are the differences of nodes. Hence the reduced block of the fibre of $i'$
equals that of $i$ when $i'-i$ is even, and its transpose when $i'-i$ is odd. In the window the fibres alternate between
one more row than column and one more column than row. A nonzero left kernel vector $\mathbf y$ of one block, transported
to every fibre of the window, is a left kernel vector on the fibres with a surplus row and a right kernel vector on the
others, with the same moment pair everywhere. By Lemma~\ref{lem:pairing} all pairings between these vectors vanish
modulo $p^2$. Extending the $n_1$ vectors on rows and the $n_2$ vectors on columns, $n_1+n_2=t_2-t_1$, to unimodular
matrices, \eqref{eq:corner} gives $v(\det B)\ge t_2-t_1$. Since $\det B=p^{2(r+m)}\det\Phi$, the theorem follows.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{The rank bound (R)}\label{sssec:rank}
For $2m<p$ write $r=hp+r_p$ with $0\le r_p<p$ (note that $k_p=\lceil jp/q\rceil$, since $0<o<q$), and put
$\mathrm{arc}_0=[0,k_p-1]$,
$\mathrm{arc}_1=[k_p,p-1]$. For $k\ge0$ and $z\in\{0,1\}$ let
\[
\begin{gathered}
 n_{k,z}=\bigl|[r,r+2m)\cap[kp+\min\mathrm{arc}_z,\,kp+\max\mathrm{arc}_z]\bigr|,\\
 n_{\rm sq}=\bigl|[\max(0,r+2m-p),\min(r,k_p))\bigr|,
\end{gathered}
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PiR}
 \Pi_R=2(r+m)-\max\bigl(m+n_{\rm sq},\ n_{0,0}+n_{1,0}+2n_{\rm sq},\ n_{h,0},\ n_{h,1},\ n_{h+1,0},\ n_{h+1,1}\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the bound (R)]
Every node is $t=i+pk$ with residue $i\in[0,p)$ and digit $k$. Write $k(i)$ for the number of common nodes in the fibre
of $i$. Since the border has length $2m<p$, this fibre contains the common digits $0,\dots,k(i)-1$ and at most one border
digit, namely $k(i)\in\{h,h+1\}$, which is a row or a column according to the parity of $i+pk(i)-r$; the step function
$V$ is constant on the fibre up to the bit $z(i)=\mathbf 1[i\ge k_p]$. Thus the reduced block of a fibre depends only
on $(k(i),z(i))$ and the role of its border digit, and its entries have the L\"owner form of Lemma~\ref{lem:moment}. A
fibre with a border row (column) has a left (right) kernel vector with nonzero moment pair; for the digit $0$ below the
pole the step function vanishes, so these fibres share the moment direction $(1,0)$, and a fibre of type $(1,0)$
without border digit has the zero block and contributes one row and one column. Choosing one such vector per fibre kills
$m+n_{\rm sq}$ rows and as many columns, which gives the saving $m+n_{\rm sq}$. Within a class of fibres of the same type
$(k,z)$ all moment pairs are equal, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:pairing} the pairings between the killed rows and columns of one
class vanish modulo $p^2$, and \eqref{eq:corner} gives a saving equal to the number of killed coordinates of the class:
$n_{k,z}$ for $k\ge1$ and $(k,z)\ne(1,0)$, and $n_{0,0}+n_{1,0}+2n_{\rm sq}$ for the class of direction $(1,0)$. Taking the
best class gives
\eqref{eq:PiR}. A deleted common node changes at most one fibre; giving it, and one further fibre to restore the
balance of rows and columns, the trivial certificate costs at most two killed coordinates of each kind, which the
smaller matrix compensates, so the debit is $0$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Separated border nodes: the bound (S)}\label{ssec:sep}
The last bound uses a different source of cancellation. Choose a window $[t_0,t_v]$ of border nodes whose residues lie
in one arc between consecutive poles, and consider a border node outside it. If the interval between the node and
$t_0$ contains a singular index, the residues modulo $p$ of $p$ times the partial sums at the two nodes differ (two
singular contributions in an interval of length less than $p$ cannot cancel). The leading coefficient in $x$ of the
bordered determinant of a fibre is this residue, so the cross product of the two bordered fibre determinants has a
nonzero leading coefficient, and the border node is eliminated against $t_0$ with no further information. Counting the
residues of the window together with the residues of the eliminated border nodes gives an explicit bound, a minimum over
the four window types $(k,z)\in\{h,h+1\}\times\{0,1\}$, which we now state.

Let $2m\le p$, and let $h$, $r_p$, the arcs $\mathrm{arc}_0$, $\mathrm{arc}_1$ and the counts $n_{k,z}$ be as in
\S\ref{sssec:rank}. For a type $(k,z)$ let $\mathcal K_{k,z}$ be the interval of the integers $kp+i$ with $i\in\mathrm{arc}_z$, and with
$i<r_p$ if $k=h+1$; the border nodes in $\mathcal K_{k,z}$ form the window, and there are $n_{k,z}$ of them. Let $n_K$ be the number
of border nodes in $\mathcal K_{k,z}$ and in its two translates $\mathcal K_{k,z}\pm p$, and put
\[
 n_\emptyset=p-|\mathcal K_{k,z}|-(2m-n_K),\qquad n_+=(n_{k,z}-m)_+ .
\]
Since $2m\le p$, each residue class receives at most one border node, so $n_K$ is the number of border nodes whose residues
are those of $\mathcal K_{k,z}$, and $n_\emptyset$ is the number of the other residue classes that receive no border node. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PiS}
 \Pi_S=\min_{(k,z)}\bigl(2r+m-n_+-(n_+-n_\emptyset)_+\bigr),
\end{equation}
where a type whose window is empty contributes $2r+m$, and (S) is the bound $e_p(r,I)\le\Pi_S+|I|$. The proof carries out
the elimination above for every border node outside the window; we refer to the formal proof for it.

\subsection{Profiles}\label{ssec:profiles}
Let $\mathcal E$ be the set of integer expressions in $r,p,m,k$ built from integer constants with $+$, multiplication by
integer constants, truncated subtraction $(X-Y)_+$, $\min$, $\max$, the distance $\|X\|_p$ of an expression $X$ to $p\Z$,
and the quantities $p\lfloor r/p\rfloor$ and $r\bmod p$. Every bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:local} lies in $\mathcal E$; for (R)
and (S) this is \eqref{eq:PiR} and \eqref{eq:PiS}, the ends of the intervals $\mathcal K_{k,z}$ being $kp=p\lfloor r/p\rfloor+(k-h)p$
plus $0$, $k_p$, $k_p-1$, $p-1$ or $r_p-1$, and for (W) the window ends are the cuts nearest to the ends of the window
range, which are expressions of the same kind. The \emph{homogenisation} $\hat\tau(\theta)$ of $\tau\in\mathcal E$ replaces $r,p,m,k$ by
$1,\theta,\alpha,\kappa\theta$, every constant by $0$, $\|X\|_p$ by $\theta\|\hat X/\theta\|$ and $p\lfloor r/p\rfloor$ by
$\theta\lfloor1/\theta\rfloor$. It is piecewise affine with rational breakpoints, finitely many in every interval bounded
away from $0$.

\begin{lemma}[homogenisation]\label{lem:homog}
For each $\tau\in\mathcal E$ there is an explicit constant $E(\tau)$, computed recursively from
$E(m)=\frac12$, $E(k)=1-\frac1q$ and the rules $E(\tau+\tau')=E(\tau)+E(\tau')$, $E(\min(\tau,\tau'))\le\max(E(\tau),E(\tau'))$, such
that $|\tau(r,p,m(r),k_p)-r\hat\tau(p/r)|\le E(\tau)$ for all $r,p\ge1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The real version of $\tau$, with the constants dropped, is positively homogeneous of degree one, and every operation is
$1$-Lipschitz in the maximum norm. The inputs differ from their homogeneous values by $|m-\alpha r|\le\frac12$ and
$|k_p-\kappa p|\le1-\frac1q$. Induct on $\tau$.
\end{proof}

The conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:local} are linear inequalities $L\ge0$ or $L>0$ in $r,p,m,k$, and homogenise to
intervals $U$ of $\theta$. At an endpoint the error $L-r\hat L(p/r)$ is a combination of $m-\alpha r$ and $k_p-\kappa p$.
With the rounding $m=\lceil\alpha r-\frac12\rceil$ the endpoints $\theta=2\alpha$ (from $2m<p$) and $\theta=(1+2\alpha)/(1+\kappa)$
(from $N\le p+k$) are \emph{exact}: the condition holds for every prime on the correct side. The other endpoints are exact
or hold outside a strip of primes of bounded width around the endpoint, on which another bound must be used.

\subsection{The checker and the content theorem}\label{ssec:checker}
For the root class $j$ let $\mathcal P_j(\theta)$ be the minimum of the homogenised bounds $\hat\tau_b(\theta)$ over the
bounds $b$ valid at $\theta$. A \emph{content table} for the frame $F_s$ and the geometry of parameter $c$ consists of the parameters $(q,\alpha,K)$
and, for every root class $j$, an increasing sequence $0<\theta_0<\theta_1<\dots<\theta_{\rm end}$ with, on each piece
$(\theta_{i-1},\theta_i]$, an affine function $\ell_i(\theta)$ and a constant $C_i\ge0$, each piece tagged with a bound $b$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:local} and, at each inexact endpoint of the validity interval of $b$, a second bound valid across it;
together with a constant $\ell_0$ for the prefix $(0,\theta_0]$ and the rational number $\Lambda_{\rm fin}$. The
\emph{checker} accepts the table if:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(C\arabic*),leftmargin=*]
\item for each shift of the table, every unit class modulo the modulus $M$ of the prime number theorem used is assigned a
 root class with a table;
\item on each piece $\ell_i$ is nonnegative, the piece lies in the validity interval of its bound, and $\hat\tau_b$ lies
 below $\ell_i$ at both ends and at both one-sided limits of each breakpoint inside the piece;
\item at each inexact endpoint the second bound is valid on both sides and lies below $\ell_i$ there, and $C_i$ exceeds
 the error constants of Lemma~\ref{lem:homog}, the debits times $K$, the strip widths times the slopes, and the trivial
 bound at the scales below an explicit threshold;
\item $\ell_0\ge2+2\alpha$, $0<\theta_0\le1$, and $\theta_{\rm end}\ge\max(q(1+2\alpha),p_0/2)$ with
 $\kappa_j\theta_{\rm end}\ge1+2\alpha$;
\item $\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_j\bigl(\ell_0\theta_0+\sum_i\int_{\theta_{i-1}}^{\theta_i}\ell_i(\theta)\,d\theta\bigr)=\Lambda_{\rm fin}$.
\end{enumerate}
All of these are comparisons of rational numbers.

\begin{theorem}[content]\label{thm:content}
Let $0<c\le1$, let $F_s$ be a frame with $s$ of denominator $q$, and suppose that the checker accepts a content table with
$s$ among its shifts and depth $K$. Then for every even $r$ and every member with $|I|\le K$,
\[
 \log\den P_{r,I}\le(\Lambda_{\rm fin}+o(1))\,r^2 .
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By definition $\den P_{r,I}=\prod_pp^{e_p(r,I)}$. Fix a piece $(\theta_{i-1},\theta_i]$ of the root class $j$ and a prime $p$ in a
unit class assigned to $j$ with $\theta_{i-1}r<p\le\theta_ir$. For $r$ beyond the threshold of (C3) the conditions of the
tagged bound hold (exactly, or outside a strip where the second bound applies), and Lemma~\ref{lem:homog} with (C2), (C3)
gives $e_p(r,I)\le r\,\ell_i(p/r)+C_i$; below the threshold the trivial bound is at most $C_i$. For $p\le\theta_0r$ the bound
(T1) gives $e_p\le\ell_0r+O(1)$, with the primes $p^2\le q(r+2m)$, the prime powers and the finitely many primes below
$p_0$ contributing $O(r^{3/2}\log r)$. For $p>\theta_{\rm end}r$, (C4) gives $N\le p$ and $k_p\ge N$, and (T5) gives $e_p=0$. By
the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions modulo $M$, for each unit class
$\sum_{\theta_{i-1}r<p\le\theta_ir,\ p\equiv\cdot}r\,\ell_i(p/r)\log p=\frac{r^2}{\varphi(M)}\int_{\theta_{i-1}}^{\theta_i}\ell_i(\theta)\,d\theta+o(r^2)$,
and the class $j$ receives $\varphi(M)/\varphi(q)$ unit classes modulo $M$, because the map sending a unit to $qs$ times
its inverse permutes the units modulo $q$. Summing and using (C5) gives the theorem; the constants $C_i$ contribute $O(r)$.
\end{proof}

The tables used in this paper are generated from the functions $\mathcal P_j$ themselves: the pieces are the maximal
intervals on which $\mathcal P_j$ is affine and attained by one bound, so that
$\Lambda_{\rm fin}=\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_j\int\mathcal P_j$ up to the prefix. Every bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:local} is available to the tables: (T1)--(T5), (LP1), (LP2), (R), (S), and the
windows (W). The windows with $n=0$ are used for $m<p$, between consecutive cuts. On $(0,\alpha]$, that is for $p\le m$,
the only other bounds are (T1) and (T1$'$); with (T1$'$) alone the profile there is the sawtooth
$2+2\alpha-\theta\|\alpha/\theta\|$, whose integral is
\[
 \int_0^\alpha\Bigl(2+2\alpha-\theta\Bigl\|\frac\alpha\theta\Bigr\|\Bigr)d\theta=2\alpha+\frac{\alpha^2}2+2\alpha^2\log2,
\]
since $\int_1^\infty\|y\|y^{-3}\,dy=\frac32-2\log2$. The windows with $n=\lfloor m/p\rfloor\ge1$ lower this profile on the
strips $\alpha/(n+1)<\theta\le\alpha/n$; the tables use them for $n\le8$, again with each window running between two
consecutive cuts. Below $\alpha/9$ a table resolves the sawtooth into teeth down to $\alpha/n_0$
($n_0=100$ for $q=4,5,7$ and $n_0=50$ for $q=3$) and ends with a constant piece on $(0,\alpha/n_0]$, which costs $\alpha^2T_{n_0}$
with $0<T_n\le1/(4n^2)$; the number $\Lambda_{\rm fin}$ is then rational, and (C5) is an identity between rational numbers.
The bounds depend on the shift $s$ only through the root class, and their profiles only through $(q,j,\alpha)$, so one
table serves all shifts with denominator $q$.

\section{Nonvanishing}\label{sec:nonvanishing}

The deduction needs, for every rational $x_0$ and every large scale, one member of the family that does not vanish at
$x_0$. For the frames of \eqref{eq:frames} this holds in characteristic zero, with a number of deleted common nodes
bounded independently of $r$; this is the statement used in Section~\ref{sec:proof}.

\subsection{Selection in characteristic zero}\label{ssec:selection}
Normalise the frame so that the divided differences of $F$ at consecutive nodes have prefix values $f_t$, with
$f_{t+1}-f_t=\Delta(t)$, and the diagonal prefix $e_t$ satisfies $e_{t+1}-e_t=-\Delta'(t)$. For $F_s$,
\[
 \Delta(z)=\omega\Bigl(\frac1{z+1}-\frac1{z+s}\Bigr)=\frac{\omega(s-1)}{(z+1)(z+s)} ,
\]
and for $F_\chi$, with $\omega=-\frac16$,
\[
 \Delta(z)=\frac{1-z}{216\,(z+1)(z+\frac13)(z+\frac12)} .
\]

\begin{theorem}[selection]\label{thm:selection}
Let $F$ be a frame of \eqref{eq:frames} with $d$ digamma terms, and consider the alternating geometry of parameter $c$. There is
$K$, depending only on $d$ and on the period $2$ of the border, such that for every large even $r$ and every $x_0\in\Q$ some
member $P_{r,I}$ with $|I|\le K$ satisfies $P_{r,I}(x_0)\ne0$.
\end{theorem}
% Lean: CatalanCore.Uniform.BlockGeom.hasNV0, with depth BlockGeom.selectionDepth (at most 64 for the two-term frames).

\begin{proof}[Sketch of proof]
Write $N=r+2m$ and $n=r+m$. By barycentric interpolation a vector in the right kernel of $\Phi_{r,\emptyset}(x_0)$ corresponds to a
pair of polynomials $(u,w)$ of degree less than $n$ with the \emph{value conditions} $w(t)=f_tu(t)$ at the $N$ nodes and the
\emph{jet conditions} $w'(t)=f_tu'(t)+(x_0-e_t)u(t)$ at the common nodes $t$, and similarly for the left kernel. Two such pairs
have zero cross product $w_1u_2-w_2u_1$: its degree is at most $2n-2$, while the value and jet conditions give $N+r=2n$ zeros
counted with multiplicity.

For a pair whose degrees are small enough, the discrete Wronskian
$W(z)=Q(z)[w(z+1)u(z)-w(z)u(z+1)]-A(z)u(z)u(z+1)$, where $\Delta=A/Q$ in lowest terms, has more prescribed zeros at the
nodes than its degree allows, and so vanishes identically. Then $w/u$ would be a rational antidifference of $\Delta$, which
is impossible: the simple pole of $\Delta$ at $-1$ is isolated in its class modulo $\Z$, because the other poles of $\Delta$
(at $-s$ for $F_s$, and at $-\frac13$ and $-\frac12$ for $F_\chi$) are not congruent to $-1$ modulo $\Z$, and clearing
denominators in $w(z+1)/u(z+1)-w(z)/u(z)=\Delta(z)$ would propagate a root of
the denominator indefinitely in one direction. Counting the zeros by chains of nodes of step $2$ bounds the dimension of
the kernel, and so the corank of $\Phi_{r,\emptyset}(x_0)$, by a number $K$ depending only on $d$ and on the number of such
chains, which is bounded independently of $r$.

Finally, the left and right kernels map to the same interpolation space, and at a common node $t$ their coordinates are the
values $u(t)$ times nonzero factors. No nonzero kernel pair has $u$ vanishing at every common node (dividing by the node
polynomial of the common nodes would give a pair of degree less than $m$ satisfying the value conditions at all $N$ nodes,
and the Wronskian argument applies again). Hence there is a set $I$ of $|I|=\corank$ common nodes such that no nonzero left
or right kernel vector vanishes on $I$. The rows outside $I$ then span the row space, the columns outside $I$ span the
column space, and the principal minor deleting $I$ is nonzero.
\end{proof}

% BlockGeom.selectionDepth.

The theorem applies to every frame of \eqref{eq:frames}; for $F_\chi$ the pole of $\Delta$ at $-1$ is again isolated in its
class modulo $\Z$, the other poles being $-\frac13$ and $-\frac12$. In the formal proof the depth is $K\le64$ for all the
frames of this paper.

\section{Analytic decay}\label{sec:decay}

In this section we bound $|P_{r,I}(x)|$ for $x$ near $\xi$. The argument is that of OpenAI \cite{OpenAI2026} for
square determinants, with three changes: the determinant has two families of nodes (rows and columns), the
comparison measure is a density on finitely many segments rather than an explicit equilibrium measure, and the
inequalities that remain are verified on a finite cover by cells. The result is one theorem for every geometry
parameter $c\le1$ and every frame of \eqref{eq:frames}.

\subsection{A contour representation}
Hermite's formula gives $\Lw_F[t,t']=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint F(z)v_t(z)v_{t'}(z)\,dz$ with $v_t(z)=1/(z-t)$, the contour enclosing
the nodes and no pole of $F$. Write $F=\sum_{u\in\mathcal T}w_u\psi(z+u)$, where $\mathcal T$ is the set of shifts of $F$ and
$\sum_uw_u=0$: for $F_s$, $\mathcal T=\{1,s\}$ with the weights $w_1=1$ and $w_s=-1$, and for $F_\chi$,
$\mathcal T=\{\frac13,\frac12,1\}$. The reflection formula $\psi(w)=\psi(1-w)-\pi\cot\pi w$ splits $F=\tilde F+E$, where
$\tilde F$ is analytic in the left half-plane and $O(1/|z|)$ there, and
\[
 E(z)=-\pi\sum_{u\in\mathcal T}w_u\cot\pi(z+u)
\]
is $1$-periodic with $|E(z)|\le C_Ee^{-2\pi|\Im z|}$ for $|\Im z|\ge1$, because the weights sum to zero. For $F_s$,
$E_s(z)=\pi[\cot\pi(z+s)-\cot\pi z]$. Let $\Gamma$ be a polygonal path, symmetric under complex conjugation, which crosses
the real axis at a point $x^*<0$ that is not a pole of $F$ (the poles are the points $-(j+u)$, $j\ge0$, $u\in\mathcal T$),
runs through the left half-plane and ends in two rays. Moving the contour to $\Gamma$ picks up the residues of $F$ at the
poles in $(x^*,0)$, and the part of the integrand with $\tilde F$ integrates to $0$ over $\Gamma$. Hence, for all integers
$t,t'\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gram}
 \Lw_F[t,t']=\int v_tv_{t'}\,dm,
\end{equation}
where $m$ is the complex measure with the mass $-\operatorname{res}_y(F)$ at each pole $y\in(x^*,0)$ of $F$ and the density
$(2\pi i)^{-1}E(z)\,dz$ on $\Gamma$. This is a Gram representation with respect to a measure with finitely many atoms and
an exponentially small density on $\Gamma$. After scaling by $r$, the atoms lie on a segment $[-r_0,0]$ with density $d$, the number of digamma
terms of $F$ (two for $F_s$, three for $F_\chi$). The separation condition on $x^*$ is a finite check on the
fractional parts of $u-x^*$, $u\in\mathcal T$.
% Lean: d is SplineData.dim, required to equal Fr.d in altGeom_hasScaledDecay (the poles -(n + u), u in T, give d atoms
% per unit length); the shifts are Frame.mu.

\subsection{Two-family Andr\'eief bound}
For a set $C''\subseteq C$ of common nodes put $\mathcal M_{C''}=\omega\,\Lw_F[C''\sqcup X',C''\sqcup Y']$. For a member with
common nodes $C'=C\setminus I$ put $R'=C'\sqcup X'$, $Q'=C'\sqcup Y'$ and $n=|C'|+m$. By multilinearity in the diagonal,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expand}
 P_{r,I}(\xi+h)=\sum_{C''\subseteq C'}h^{|C'|-|C''|}\det\mathcal M_{C''} .
\end{equation}
Andr\'eief's identity \cite{Forrester2018Andreief} for the two families $(v_t)_{t\in R'}$ and $(v_{t'})_{t'\in Q'}$ gives
\[
 \det\Lw_F[R',Q']=\frac1{n!}\int\det[v_t(z_i)]\,\det[v_{t'}(z_i)]\prod_{i=1}^ndm(z_i),
\]
and Cauchy's determinant evaluates both factors. Taking absolute values (see \cite{SaffTotik} for the
logarithmic potential theory behind what follows),
\begin{multline*}
 |\det\Lw_F[R',Q']|\le\frac1{n!}\int\prod_{i<i'}|z_i-z_{i'}|^2\,\\
 \prod_{\mathcal F\in\{R',Q'\}}\frac{\prod_{t<t'\in\mathcal F}|t'-t|}{\prod_{i,\,t\in\mathcal F}|z_i-t|}\ \prod_id|m|(z_i).
\end{multline*}
The integrand is the exponential of minus a discrete logarithmic energy: the free points $z_i$ repel each other with
weight $1$, each node family repels itself with weight $\frac12$ and attracts the free points with weight $1$, and the
density $|E|\approx e^{-2\pi|\Im z|}$ acts as an external field. Because the rows and columns differ only in the
interleaved border, the two node families have the same macroscopic density: after scaling by $r$, both are
\[
 \nu_c=\mathrm{Leb}|_{[0,1]}+\tfrac12\mathrm{Leb}|_{[1,1+c]},
\]
of mass $b=1+c/2$.

\subsection{The energy certificate}
For a regularisation radius $\rho>0$ let $k_\rho(z,w)=-\frac12\log(|z-w|^2+\rho^2)\le-\log|z-w|$.

\begin{lemma}[conditional positive definiteness]\label{lem:cpd}
For points $z_1,\dots,z_n\in\C$ and real numbers $a_1,\dots,a_n$ with $\sum_ia_i=0$,
$\sum_{i,j}a_ia_jk_\rho(z_i,z_j)\ge0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Frullani's integral $\log y=\int_0^\infty(e^{-u}-e^{-yu})\,du/u$ ($y>0$) and $\sum_ia_i=0$ give
$\sum_{i,j}a_ia_j(-\log(|z_i-z_j|^2+\rho^2))=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-u\rho^2}}u\sum_{i,j}a_ia_je^{-u|z_i-z_j|^2}du$,
and Gaussian kernels are positive definite: expanding $e^{2u\langle z,z'\rangle}$ in powers and the binomial theorem
write the inner sum as a convergent sum of squares.
\end{proof}

A \emph{certificate measure} is a probability measure $\sigma$, symmetric under conjugation, carried by finitely many
segments $[A_i,B_i]$ (with rational endpoints) contained in $\Gamma\cup[-r_0,0]\cup[0,1]$, on each of which it has the
density $f_i(y)\,dy$ in the parameter $y\in[0,1]$ of the segment, with $f_i$ a polynomial with rational coefficients and
$0\le f_i\le M_i$ for a rational $M_i$. We write $\Theta_\sigma=\sum_iM_i/|B_i-A_i|$ for the sum of the bounds of its densities
with respect to arclength. Comparing the free points and the deleted nodes with $b\sigma$ through
Lemma~\ref{lem:cpd} bounds the free--free energy from below by a sum of one-point terms and the constant $b^2I(\sigma)$;
Riemann-sum comparisons replace the node sums by the potential $U^{\nu_c}$ and the node self-energy by $I(\nu_c)$, up to
$O(r\log r)$. With the external field one arrives at the function
\[
 g(z)=b\,U^\sigma(z)-U^{\nu_c}(z)+(1-\varepsilon)\pi|\Im z|
\]
for a small fixed $\varepsilon>0$, where $U^\sigma(z)=\int\log\frac1{|z-w|}\,d\sigma(w)$ is the logarithmic potential of
$\sigma$, and $U^{\nu_c}$ that of $\nu_c$. A certificate consists of $\sigma$ and
rational numbers $m'$, $g_{\min}$, $J$, $I_\sigma$, $I_\nu$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item $g\ge m'$ on $\Gamma$;
\item $g\ge g_{\min}$ on $[0,1]$, the scaled position of the deleted common nodes;
\item $J\le\int_{-r_0}^0\min(g(x)-m',0)\,dx$;
\item $I(\sigma)\le I_\sigma$ and $I(\nu_c)\ge I_\nu$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[decay and window]\label{thm:decay}
Let $0<c\le1$, let $F$ be a frame of \eqref{eq:frames} with $d$ digamma terms whose poles are separated from $x^*$, and let
$(\sigma,m',g_{\min},J,I_\sigma,I_\nu)$ be a certificate for $\Gamma$ and $\nu_c$. Let $\rho>0$, $\delta_\rho\ge\pi\rho\,\Theta_\sigma$,
$\delta_{\rm m}>0$ and $\delta_J>0$ be rational, and put
\[
 \delta=3b^2\delta_\rho+4b^2\delta_{\rm m}+2d\,\delta_J .
\]
If $D$ and $\gamma$ are rational numbers with
\[
 D\le2(b\,m'+d\,J)-b^2I_\sigma+I_\nu-\delta,\qquad \gamma\ge2(m'-g_{\min}),
\]
then for every depth $K$, every member of the alternating geometry of parameter $c$ and every real $h$ with
$|h|\le e^{-\gamma r}$,
\[
 |P_{r,I}(\xi+h)|\le\exp\bigl(-(D-o(1))r^2\bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

The loss $\delta$ comes from replacing $\sigma$ by finitely many atoms in the comparison with the free points: the
regularisation radius $\rho$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:cpd} and the mesh of the atoms lower $m'$ by $b(\delta_\rho+\delta_{\rm m})$ and
raise $I_\sigma$ by $\delta_\rho+2\delta_{\rm m}$, and Darboux sums for the integral in (c) lower $J$ by $\delta_J$; inserted in
the expression for $D$, these changes give $\delta$. The tolerances $\delta_{\rm m}$ and $\delta_J$ may be any positive rational
numbers, since the atoms can be taken as fine as needed. In the certificates of this paper $\rho=10^{-12}$ and
$\delta<10^{-9}$.

The node energy $I(\nu_c)$ is a finite sum $\sum_ka_kH(y_k)$, $H(y)=\frac12y^2\log|y|-\frac34y^2$, over the rational
differences $y_k$ of the endpoints of the two pieces of $\nu_c$, and the lower bound $I_\nu$ is checked with rational
enclosures of the logarithms (by the series of $\operatorname{artanh}$ and a reduction to $[1,2)$). The energy
$I(\sigma)$ is a finite sum of pair energies of polynomial densities on segments, each a closed form of the type
polynomial times logarithm plus arctangent, bounded in the same way. The conditions (a)--(c) are statements about
$g$ on sets of positive measure, and are proved on a cover by cells.

\subsection{The cover by cells}\label{ssec:slope-cert}
Each of the conditions (a)--(c) is checked on a finite cover by cells with exact rational arithmetic. On a cell, the
potential $U^\sigma$ is split into the contribution of the segments of $\sigma$ near the cell and the rest. Near segments
are bounded by a Taylor polynomial in the cell variable with an explicit remainder, or, for a cell that meets the line
of a segment beyond its end, by the same bound for the segment with its orientation reversed; at a corner of $\Gamma$
the two incident segments are treated together. Far segments are bounded by a multipole expansion with an explicit
tail. The potential $U^{\nu_c}$ is an explicit elementary function and is bounded above by monotonicity on the cell.
For (a) the cells are intervals of the arclength on each edge of $\Gamma$, and the bound is proved on a horizontal
neighbourhood of the edge of fixed width; beyond the last vertex the ray is handled by an analytic tail bound. For (b)
the cells are intervals of $[0,1]$. For (c) the integrand is bounded above on each cell of $[-r_0,0]$ by a constant or by
a chord, and $J$ is the sum of the integrals of these majorants.

\begin{proposition}[certified constants]\label{prop:cert}
For each row of Table~\ref{tab:data} there is a certificate measure $\sigma$ on a polygon $\Gamma$, with a cover by cells
accepted by the checks above, for which Theorem~\ref{thm:decay} gives the constants $D$ and $\gamma$ of the table.
\end{proposition}

All inequalities are verified by the Lean kernel (\S\ref{sec:lean}); the certificate data (the polygon, the density
$\sigma$ and the cells) were computed with floating-point optimisation and interval arithmetic in Arb \cite{Arb}, and are
checked in Lean with rational arithmetic only. The data play no role in the proof beyond being accepted.

\begin{remark}[how good a certificate can be]\label{rem:certlimit}
For a multiplier $B$ the certificate meets the exponent $B$ exactly when $B(D-\Lambda)-\gamma\ge0$. This quantity is a concave
function of $\sigma$, and its maximum over all probability measures on $\Gamma\cup[-r_0,0]\cup[0,1]$ is attained at the
measure $\sigma^*$ for which $b\sigma^*$ is the weighted equilibrium measure of the constrained energy problem on this set with
the external field $(1-\varepsilon)\pi|\Im z|-U^{\nu_c}$ and the upper constraint $d\,dx$ on the segment \cite{SaffTotik}.
Weak duality expresses the deficit of any certificate as a sum of nonnegative terms, the first of which is the energy of
$2Bb(\sigma-\sigma^*)$, the difference between $2Bb\sigma$ and the optimal dual measure $2Bb\sigma^*$. Thus a certificate
close to the best possible one must be close to $\sigma^*$ in energy, and $g-m'$ is then a small ripple on the support of
$\sigma^*$;
this is why a pointwise verification on a cover is needed. This remark is not used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
and is not part of the formalization.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Node removal and the window}
The argument applies to every subset $C''\subseteq C'$ of the common nodes: removing common nodes turns them into points of
the configuration that are not integrated over, and only common nodes can be removed, because a node removed from one
family only would leave an uncancelled term of weight $\frac12$. By condition (b) one obtains
\[
 |\det\mathcal M_{C''}|\le\exp\bigl(-(D-o(1))r^2+\gamma r\,|C'\setminus C''|\bigr),
\]
uniformly in $C''$. Inserting this into \eqref{eq:expand} with $|h|e^{\gamma r}\le1$ gives Theorem~\ref{thm:decay}: the sum has
at most $2^{|C'|}=e^{O(r)}$ terms, each at most $e^{-(D-o(1))r^2}$. The deletion of at most $K$ common nodes in a member
changes the energy by $O(r)$.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:proof}

The three estimates combine as follows.

\begin{theorem}[deduction]\label{thm:deduction}
Let $F$ be a frame of \eqref{eq:frames} with its geometry and a depth $K$ such that some member $P_{r,I}$, $|I|\le K$, is
nonzero at every rational point for every large $r$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:selection}). Suppose that the content constant
$\Lambda$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:content} and the constants $D$, $\gamma$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:decay} satisfy $\Lambda<D$. Then
$\mu(\xi)\le\gamma/(D-\Lambda)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\eta=D-\Lambda$, let $\mu_0>\mu_1>\gamma/\eta$, and suppose $|\xi-a/b|<b^{-\mu_0}$ with $b$ large; put $L=\log b$. Let
$r$ be the least admissible scale with $r\ge\mu_1L/\gamma$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:selection} with $x_0=a/b$ there is a member
with $P_{r,I}(a/b)\ne0$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:integrality}, $|\den(P_{r,I})\,P_{r,I}(a/b)|\ge b^{-r}=e^{-rL}$.
Write $a/b=\xi+h$ with $|h|<e^{-\mu_0L}\le e^{-\gamma r}$ (as $\gamma r\le\mu_1L+O(1)<\mu_0L$ for large $b$). By
Theorems~\ref{thm:content} and~\ref{thm:decay},
$|\den(P_{r,I})\,P_{r,I}(a/b)|\le e^{(\Lambda+o(1))r^2}e^{-(D-o(1))r^2}=e^{-(\eta-o(1))r^2}$. Comparing,
$(\eta-o(1))r^2\le rL$, whereas $\eta r\ge(\mu_1\eta/\gamma)L$ with $\mu_1\eta/\gamma>1$; this is a contradiction for large
$b$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
For each row of Table~\ref{tab:main} the frame, the geometry parameter and the constants are those of
Table~\ref{tab:data}. The content constant is given by Theorem~\ref{thm:content} with the content table of the formal proof
(for $L(2,\chi_{-3})$, by Section~\ref{sec:chi3-quarter}), the decay constants by Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}, and
nonvanishing by Theorem~\ref{thm:selection}. In every row $\gamma/(D-\Lambda)<B$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:deduction}, applied at
the even scales, gives $\mu(\xi)\le B$. Since $\xi$ is a nonzero rational multiple of
$\Psi(s,t)$ (\eqref{eq:frames}, and $\xi_\chi=\frac1{36}\Psi(\frac13,\frac23)$), this proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[relation to linear independence criteria]
The deduction is a one-dimensional version of the familiar scheme behind the criteria of Nesterenko
\cite{Nesterenko85} and their use by Ball and Rivoal \cite{BallRivoal01}: the nonzero integer
$b^{|C\setminus I|}\den(P_{r,I})\,P_{r,I}(a/b)$ plays the role of a small linear form with integer coefficients. The difference is that
the lower bound for the form comes from an arithmetic nonvanishing statement rather than from an analytic asymptotic,
and that the size $r$ of the determinant is chosen in terms of $b$.
\end{remark}

\section{The families}\label{sec:families}

We collect the facts specific to each row of Table~\ref{tab:data}. In every case the frame is $F_s$ of \eqref{eq:frames} with
$s=k/q$, $\omega=q^{-2}$, and $\xi_s=-q^{-2}\Psi(\frac kq,1)$; the content table, the certificate measure and the cover are
the data of the formal proof.

\subsection{The shifts $\frac14$ and $\frac34$}
From $\psi'(\frac14)=\pi^2+8G$, $\psi'(\frac34)=\pi^2-8G$ and $\psi'(1)=\pi^2/6$,
\[
 \xi_{1/4}=-\frac1{16}\Psi(\tfrac14,1)=-\lambda_+,\qquad \xi_{3/4}=-\frac1{16}\Psi(\tfrac34,1)=\lambda_- .
\]
The geometry parameter is $c=\frac15$, so $\alpha=\frac1{10}$. The two shifts $s=\frac k4$, $k=1,3$, have root classes $j\equiv kp^{-1}\pmod4$,
which exchange the two classes of primes modulo $4$; by the transport of \S\ref{ssec:checker} they have the same content
constant, and the same certificate serves both, since the decay depends on the shift only through the separation of the
poles from the contour.

\subsection{The shifts $\frac13$ and $\frac23$}\label{sec:thirds}
Write $L=L(2,\chi_{-3})$. From $\psi'(\tfrac13)=9\sum_{n\ge0}(3n+1)^{-2}$, $\psi'(\tfrac23)=9\sum_{n\ge0}(3n+2)^{-2}$ and
$\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2}=\pi^2/6$ one gets
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trigamma-thirds}
 \psi'(\tfrac13)=\frac{2\pi^2}3+\frac92L,\qquad \psi'(\tfrac23)=\frac{2\pi^2}3-\frac92L
\end{equation}
(the sum of the two is $\pi^2/\sin^2(\pi/3)=4\pi^2/3$, and their difference is $9L$), hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-thirds}
 \xi_{1/3}=-\frac12\Bigl(L+\frac{\pi^2}9\Bigr),\qquad \xi_{2/3}=\frac12\Bigl(L-\frac{\pi^2}9\Bigr).
\end{equation}
The geometry parameter is $c=\frac3{10}$. The content table uses the primes in the classes modulo $6$; the two root classes
$j=1,2$ correspond to first poles near $p/3$ and $2p/3$.

\subsection{The shifts $\frac k5$}\label{sec:fifths}
For $1\le k\le4$ the number $\Psi(\frac k5,1)=\psi'(\frac k5)-\zeta(2)$ is a rational linear combination of the values
$L(2,\chi)$ for the Dirichlet characters $\chi$ modulo $5$, since
$\psi'(\frac k5)=25\sum_{n\equiv k\ (5)}n^{-2}=\frac{25}4\sum_\chi\bar\chi(k)L(2,\chi)$. The geometry parameter is $c=\frac29$.
The four members share one content table, indexed by the four root classes, and one certificate.

\subsection{The shifts $\frac k7$}\label{sec:sevenths}
For $1\le k\le6$, likewise, $\psi'(\frac k7)-\zeta(2)$ is a combination of the values $L(2,\chi)$, $\chi\bmod7$, and of $\pi^2$.
The geometry parameter is $c=\frac16$ and there are six root classes. Here $D-\Lambda$ is small, about $2.7\cdot10^{-3}$, and
the exponent is correspondingly large.

\section{Limits of the method}\label{sec:limits}

The exponents are determined by three constants through $\gamma/(D-\Lambda)$. Since $\partial\mu/\partial D=-\gamma/\eta^2$, the
exponent is most sensitive to the decay where $\eta$ is small: for $G\pm5\pi^2/48$, $\gamma/\eta^2\approx137$, and for the
shifts $\frac k7$ it exceeds $10^6$. On the arithmetic side, the profiles $\mathcal P_j$ are minima of the bounds of
Theorem~\ref{thm:local}, and every further local bound lowers $\Lambda$ by the integral of the amount by which it lies below
$\mathcal P_j$; the content constant of the method is the integral of the true exponent $e_p$, which the local bounds approximate
from above. On the analytic side, Remark~\ref{rem:certlimit} explains why a certificate of the present form cannot do
better than the constrained equilibrium measure on the chosen contour; a larger decay constant needs a different contour or a
different treatment of the energy problem. The geometry parameter $c$ trades the two against each other: a longer border
increases $D$, through the larger node measure, and $\Lambda$, through the border rows and columns of the assignment
bound.
